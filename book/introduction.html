Who we are, and why this book exists

We don't travel to see different things; we travel to see things differently.

We're Mary-Ann and Neil — two slightly weathered humans, one tired but loyal Land Cruiser, and a ground tent we keep insisting is "actually quite comfortable." Together we've been pottering around Southern Africa for longer than is strictly sensible.

Who we are

Mary-Ann was born in Zimbabwe and came out explorer-shaped. Her late husband was a mining manager, which meant a life parked on mine sites from Vredendal to Eswatini to Indonesia — the kind of CV that breeds a certain comfort with rough roads and surprise weather. She and the Land Cruiser have been overlanding together since 2010, including a solo year all the way up to Tanzania. Hand her a corrugated gravel road and she lights up. Hand her tarmac and she's a dragon slayer with no dragons to slay.

Neil studied philosophy and religion, then somehow ended up in accounting and corporate governance — which is either a beautiful arc or a cautionary tale, depending on the lighting. Ex-surfer, ex-endurance runner, ex-city boy. His late wife was a clinical psychologist, which possibly explains a lot. He only discovered 4x4 travel in 2023, post-retirement, behind the wheel of a Pajero SWB, and has since been described by a fellow traveller in Kruger as a "hippy bushranger." He's still deciding how he feels about that.

We met in November 2023 on a Mountain Passes South Africa guided tour to the Wild Coast — both widowed, both a bit raw, both there for the road. The road did its work.

The dynamic, in short: Mary-Ann is the Zen one who loves untamed tracks and the occasional productive getting-lost. Neil is the worry pot who has already mentally inventoried the recovery gear, double-checked the diesel, and is now wondering whether the tyre pressure is philosophically correct. Somehow this works.

How we travel

Solo, slowly, and in a ground tent with stretchers because we are stubborn and old-school. We prefer to stay and explore rather than stop and go — settle in for a few nights, walk the place, watch the light change, drink the coffee (and the wine) properly. Game reserves and remote corners pull us hardest.

The expedition ahead

From 2028 we plan a long journey through Southern and East Africa. Its length is still open: one year, two, or what the road decides. This library, and this book, are how we prepare for it.

How to use this book

Emergencies first

Emergency Numbers and Emergency Procedures follow straight after this introduction, so they can be found without looking.

Part 1 · Why and how we travel

The thinking behind the journey: the Manifesto and its commentary, a history of overlanding and of the Land Cruiser, and the expedition as a system. For home, or by the fire.

Part 3 · In the field

For the roadside: how to fix, check and decide. Each field chapter ends with a ruled page for pencil notes; bring them home, they go back into the library.

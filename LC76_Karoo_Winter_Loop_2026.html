Trip Overview

A slow winter circle through the heart of the Karoo and Bushmanland — succulent plains, dolerite koppies, dry pans and some of the darkest skies in the country. The route runs almost entirely on gravel, opening in the Cederberg at the one fixed booking, climbing north through the Hantam into Bushmanland, then swinging east and south through the Upper Karoo before dropping off the Roggeveld escarpment into the Tankwa for the run home. The rhythm is deliberately unhurried: two to four nights at every stop, self-sufficient under canvas, buying food and topping up water as you pass through the towns.

The loop now runs anticlockwise, opening at the one confirmed booking rather than building toward it. Enjo is Day 1, which makes the Cederberg the shakedown leg — close enough to home that a problem found in the first 48 hours is still recoverable. The Tankwa and Kagga Kamma cluster, previously the opening, becomes the closing run. The two longest legs are Verneukpan to Stuurmansfontein and Melton Wold to Merweville; everything else is comfortably inside half a day.

⚑

Verneukpan needs a phone call to arrange access and check the pan surface; Gannabos and Stuurmansfontein are single-party farm stays and go early.

Geology & Fossils — The Floor of an Ancient World

Almost everything you drive across on this trip is the Karoo Supergroup — a single, vast pile of sedimentary rock that covers roughly two-thirds of South Africa. It was laid down over about 120 million years, from around 300 to 180 million years ago (Ma), in a great inland basin while this part of Gondwana drifted off the South Pole. Read from the bottom up, it is the story of a continent thawing out of an ice age, flooding, drying into endless river floodplains, and finally being buried under lava as Gondwana tore apart.

Karoo Supergroup
~300–180 Ma
Covers ~⅔ of South Africa. The flat plains, the layered cuttings and the koppies are all chapters of the same book.
The Great Dying
~252 Ma
Earth's largest mass extinction (~90% of species). The Karoo's Beaufort Group holds the world's best land record of it — a global reference for science.
Dolerite
The koppies
~183-Ma magma squeezed between the layers as sills and dykes. Harder than the mudstone around it, so it caps every flat-topped hill you'll see.
Fossil country
Therapsids
The "mammal-like reptiles" that preceded the dinosaurs are found in extraordinary density in the Beaufort mudstones beneath your wheels.
Reading the layers
Layer (oldest first)AgeWhat it records
Dwyka~300 MaGlacial tillite — ice sheets when this land sat near the South Pole.
Ecca~280 MaBlack shales of a vast inland sea; source of the Karoo's coal and shale gas.
Beaufort~265–250 MaRiver-floodplain mudstones — the fossil reptile beds and the end-Permian extinction record.
Stormberg~230–190 MaSandstones and the pale Clarens "cave sandstone"; early dinosaurs.
Drakensberg basalt~183 MaFlood lavas that buried it all as Gondwana broke up. The dolerite below is its plumbing.
⛰
On the ground: every flat-topped koppie and "ironstone" ridge is dolerite-capped; the open plains between are soft Beaufort mudstone. The fossil footprint surface at Gansfontein near Fraserburg (a short detour from the Sutherland leg) is one of the most accessible windows into this world. Note: Ma = million years ago.
05

Biomes & Flora — Two Karoos

The route crosses the meeting place of two great arid biomes. The Succulent Karoo in the west (the Tankwa, Cederberg fringe and Hantam) is a winter-rainfall world and one of only a handful of arid biodiversity hotspots on Earth — around 6,300 plant species, some 40% of them found nowhere else, and the richest succulent flora on the planet. The Nama-Karoo of the interior (Bushmanland and the Upper Karoo) is a summer-rainfall dwarf shrubland — sparse, grey-green "Karoo bossies" and grass over huge distances, deceptively rich underfoot.

Succulent Karoo
Global hotspot
~6,300 species, ~40% endemic. The only arid-zone hotspot on Earth. Tankwa, Cederberg edge, Hantam.
Nama-Karoo
Dwarf shrubland
Hardy shrublets and grasses across Bushmanland and the Upper Karoo. Built for drought and frost.
Quiver tree
Kokerboom
The aloe that isn't a tree. Groves around Loeriesfontein and Nieuwoudtville; San hollowed the branches for arrow quivers.
Spring flowers
Late Aug–Sept
Rain-dependent daisies, vygies and bulbs. Your timing sits right at the front edge of the display.

Nieuwoudtville, a short hop from Calvinia, is often called the bulb capital of the world for its unmatched density of geophyte species. The Biedouw Valley (Enjo) and the Hantam plains can both flower spectacularly in a good year.

🌼
Flower window: displays open from late August and need recent rain plus sun by mid-morning to perform. Check the seasonal flower line before committing to the Calvinia/Nieuwoudtville day trip — and remember flowers face the sun, so drive with the sun behind you for the best show.
06

Wildlife — Sparse, Specialised, Worth the Patience

Karoo wildlife rewards stillness rather than the big-game checklist. Gemsbok, springbok, kudu, steenbok and klipspringer hold the plains and koppies; after dark the Karoo belongs to bat-eared and Cape fox, aardvark, aardwolf, caracal and porcupine. The birding is genuinely special — this is the global heartland of Africa's larks, with several species found almost nowhere else, plus Verreaux's (black) eagle on the cliffs, blue crane on the plains, and the great bustards and korhaans striding the veld.

Karoo icon
Riverine rabbit
Critically endangered and found only in central-Karoo riverine scrub — one of the world's rarest mammals. Largely nocturnal; a privilege to glimpse.
Birding
Lark capital
Karoo, red, Sclater's and other larks; Ludwig's & kori bustard, Karoo korhaan, pale chanting goshawk, ostrich. Tankwa is a top arid-birding spot.
Reptiles
Tortoise central
The Karoo and Namaqualand have the highest tortoise diversity on Earth. Also puff adder and Cape cobra — watch where you step and reach.
Best viewing
Night drives
Melton Wold runs guided night drives (aardvark, bat-eared fox possible). Dawn and dusk are the active windows everywhere.
🦌
Driving + wildlife = the real risk. Kudu, antelope and stray livestock on unfenced gravel are the most likely hazard of the whole trip, and they move at dusk and dawn. Plan to be in camp before dark and avoid night driving on the gravel — it's the single biggest safety lever you have out here.
07

People & Heritage — Deep Time to Radio Telescopes

The Karoo's human story is as layered as its rock. The San — among the oldest continuous cultures on Earth — left rock paintings in the sandstone of the Cederberg and rock engravings (petroglyphs) pecked into the dark dolerite of the central Karoo. Khoekhoe herders followed, and from the 1700s the trekboers — semi-nomadic Dutch-descended pastoralists — pushed in with fat-tailed sheep, founding the merino-wool culture that still defines the region's towns.

First peoples
San rock art
Paintings in Cederberg sandstone; engravings on central-Karoo dolerite, including near Stuurmansfontein. Irreplaceable.
Settler heritage
Corbelled houses
Domed dry-stone dwellings (klipkoepelhuise) built by 1800s trekboers where there was no timber — unique to the Carnarvon–Williston–Fraserburg triangle. Stuurmansfontein is a preserved one you sleep in.
Town culture
Wool & water
Victorian streetscapes, NG churches and sheep economies — Calvinia, Loxton, Sutherland, Williston. Wupperthal in the Cederberg is a 19th-century Moravian mission.
Modern layer
SKA
The Square Kilometre Array radio telescope is rising near Carnarvon, inside a legally protected radio-quiet zone.
🪨
Rock-art etiquette: never touch, wet, chalk or rub paintings or engravings — skin oils and water destroy them. Visit only with the landowner's permission or a guide, and do not publish or share exact panel locations. These sites survive precisely because they stay quiet.
08

Dark Skies & Astronomy

The Karoo has some of the darkest, clearest skies on Earth — almost no light pollution, very low humidity and high ground. It's why Sutherland hosts the South African Astronomical Observatory (SAAO) and the Southern African Large Telescope (SALT), the largest single optical telescope in the Southern Hemisphere, and why the Square Kilometre Array (SKA) radio telescope sits out near Carnarvon. On this trip the pans and remote camps — Verneukpan, Stuurmansfontein, Skurweberg and Tankwa — are every bit as dark; you simply look up.

Sutherland
SALT & SAAO
Day tour of the telescopes plus a guided town night-sky session — book both ahead. Winter nights are long and crystal clear (and bitterly cold).
New moon
Day 4
Darkest skies of the trip, and it lands inside the Enjo stay. Get out on the farm both of those nights.
Full moon + eclipse
~Day 19
A partial lunar eclipse visible from South Africa falls in the early hours as you reach Merweville. Bright moon, but a real spectacle — worth setting an alarm for.
Sky quality
Among the best
The Milky Way core sits well placed in the winter evening sky. A red head-torch and 20 minutes of dark adaption transform the view.
✦
Moon-aware planning (derived from the two verified 2026 phase anchors held in the private annex; treat as approximate): the trip opens and closes dark and is bright in the middle. Days 1–5 (Enjo) sit on the new moon — the darkest evenings you will get. Days 11–14 (Verneukpan) run a waxing gibbous that sets after midnight, so the pre-dawn hours stay properly dark; worth the cold. The moon peaks full around Day 19 and stays bright through Sutherland (Days 21–24) — which is fine, because Sutherland's value is the telescopes, not naked-eye darkness. It then wanes steadily, so Tankwa (Days 24–27) and Kagga Kamma (Days 27–30) get progressively darker evenings and close the trip strongly. Abbreviations: SAAO = SA Astronomical Observatory; SALT = Southern African Large Telescope; SKA = Square Kilometre Array.

Leg 1 — Cederberg & Hantam

The opening leg, anchored on the one confirmed booking. Enjo is close enough to home that this doubles as the shakedown — anything the new Victron stack, the load plan or the tent does badly, it does here, while Clanwilliam is still an hour away. From the Biedouw the route climbs the escarpment into the Hantam, where the quiver trees start and the towns get very small.

Leg 2 — Bushmanland & Upper Karoo

The remotest stretch, and the one that needs the most planning. Bushmanland is genuinely empty — long horizons, tiny towns, no Toyota within several hundred kilometres (see Breakdown & Service). Fill fuel and water at Brandvlei before the pan, and again at Carnarvon afterwards.

4 · Verneukpan — near Brandvlei

Day 11–14 · 3 nights
−30.1409, 21.0573 · access via Brandvlei · arrange permission locally before you go
Facilities
None. Wild camp on the pan edge. No water, no ablutions, no shade, no signal. Every litre and every amp-hour comes with you, and everything leaves with you.
Explore
The pan itself is the point. Malcolm Campbell ran Bluebird here in 1929 chasing the land-speed record — the measured course is still traceable and the story is worth reading before you arrive. Absolute silence, a horizon with nothing on it, and arguably the darkest sky of the whole route. Walk out onto the surface at dusk; do not drive onto it without checking the crust, especially after rain.
Access
Via Brandvlei. The pan surface is hard when dry and a trap when wet — check locally before committing the vehicle, and camp on the edge rather than the middle.
Drive in
Loeriesfontein → Brandvlei → Verneukpan ≈ 220 km / ~4 hrs. Fill everything in Brandvlei.

5 · Stuurmansfontein Corbelled House

Day 14–16 · 2 nights
−30.9557, 21.6820 · Charmaine / Piet Botha 072 352 8070 · turnoff on the R63, ~30 km from Carnarvon, then a farm track in
Facilities
A restored 19th-century corbelled stone house, sole occupancy — you have it to yourselves. No electricity, no cell signal, no wi-fi. Gas fridge and stove, cast-iron Dover stove, candles, outdoor shower with hot water, braai, swimming hole. You brief at reception and then drive out to the house alone.
Explore
The building is the exhibit — corbelled houses are a Karoo response to a treeless landscape, built entirely of stacked stone with no roof timber, and very few survive in this condition. Bushman rock art and engravings nearby, ask the hosts. Carnarvon for supplies. This is the closest you get to the SKA / MeerKAT radio-telescope reserve, which is also why the radio silence out here is real and enforced.
Access
Signed off the R63. Farm track for the last stretch — high clearance is comfortable, and the hosts will tell you the current state.
Drive in
Verneukpan → Vanwyksvlei → Carnarvon → Stuurmansfontein ≈ 300 km / ~5 hrs. The longest leg of the trip — start early, and note there is no fuel between Brandvlei and Carnarvon.

Camping on a working historic guest farm — lawns, mature trees, power on the stands, hot ablutions, pool and a genuinely old-fashioned pub. Meals available if you book them. The one stop on this leg where you can properly restock, recharge and do laundry.

Leg 3 — Roggeveld, Tankwa & Home

The closing leg turns south and west onto the Roggeveld escarpment, then drops off it into the Tankwa for the last two stops. This is the scenic payoff of the whole loop — Ouberg and Katbakkies in three days — and also the coldest ground you will camp on. Sutherland at altitude in winter is a different proposition from Bushmanland.

7 · Merweville

Day 19–21 · 2 nights
−32.6687, 21.5114 · Karoo Stilte 084 588 7211 · Springbok Lodge 083 255 6931
Facilities
Guesthouse, not camping — two nights under a roof, deliberately placed after the hardest stretch. Both options are self-catering village houses with braai facilities; Karoo Stilte is a three-bedroom cottage, Springbok Lodge is the larger and better-known of the two. Book ahead, the village is tiny.
Explore
Merweville is a near-abandoned Karoo dorp with a spectacular oversized NG Kerk and a population that has collapsed to a few dozen — the emptiness is the attraction, and it is a good place to do nothing for 48 hours. Teekloof Pass is an easy day trip south toward Beaufort West. Beaufort West itself is the nearest full resupply and the nearest Toyota.
Access
Gravel in from the Fraserburg or Prince Albert Road side. Straightforward in dry conditions.
Drive in
Melton Wold → Loxton → Fraserburg → Merweville ≈ 250 km / ~4.5 hrs, largely gravel.

8 · Sutherland

Day 21–24 · 3 nights
−32.4081, 20.6589 · Sterland 082 556 9589 · Uitkomst Guest Farm 066 371 5996
Facilities
Two camps worth booking, pick one:
Sterland (Piet Retief St, in town) — demarcated stands screened by mature trees, power and water on the stand, braais, notably good ablutions, pool. Jurg's stargazing evenings run on site, which is the reason to choose it. Walking distance to town.

Passes & Roads

This loop is strung together by mountain passes, and they are a large part of why it is worth doing. Most are straightforward gravel that the LC76 will not notice. Two are not: Ouberg is genuinely steep, and Gannaga's approaches turn to grease when wet. Both are on the closing leg, in the coldest week, which is when this section matters.

▲
Ouberg Pass — read before you drive it. It climbs (or in your direction, drops) roughly 820 m over 10.4 km, averaging about 1:13 with the steepest pitches near 1:6, summiting around 1,402 m. It is gravel throughout, erosion-damaged in places, and is consistently described as a 4×4 or high-clearance route rather than an all-car pass. From the summit it is about 40 km back into Sutherland, so committing to the descent is a real decision, not a casual one.

The winter problem: this area sees regular snowfall and conditions change fast at altitude. Wet or iced, the descent is a different road. Ask in Sutherland on the morning you leave — and if the answer is doubtful, take Verlatenkloof (R354) down to Matjiesfontein and come into the Tankwa the long way instead. The pass is a highlight, not an obligation.
◈
Gannaga Pass: the road surface itself is wide with good run-off and handles rain better than you would expect — but the approach sections on either side get extremely muddy and slippery. After heavy rain it is a 4WD proposition or a no. Quality also varies a lot with when it was last graded.

Weather & Season

August is deep Karoo winter: dry, clear, superb for gravel and stargazing — and cold. Daytime is pleasant (often 16–22 °C) but nights are hard frost, especially at altitude on the Roggeveld around Sutherland (~1,450 m) and exposed on Verneukpan, where −5 °C or lower and snow are both possible. Ouberg Pass sees regular winter snow — that is the one weather event that can close the planned line between Sutherland and Tankwa. Wind on the open pans can be fierce.

❄
Pack for serious cold: proper sleeping bags rated well below freezing, an insulated mat, beanies and a warm layer for the evenings. The OzTent handles wind well but stake and brace it hard at Verneukpan and Sutherland.

The upside: the back end of winter is the start of the spring flower season in the Hantam — the Biedouw Valley (Enjo) and Nieuwoudtville (Gannabos) are two of the best flower grounds in the country, and this route puts you in both in the opening week. Check the flower line before departure; in a good season it changes what the first eight days are worth.

Gravel & Vehicle Notes

Roughly 80% of this route is gravel — corrugations, loose surfaces and the occasional rocky section, but no serious 4×4 obstacles on the planned line. The LC76 on OME is well within its element; the main wear factors are corrugation fatigue and tyre damage, both managed by pressure and pace.

Tyre pressures
Set from the slip
Weigh loaded on Day 1 at the public weighbridge before Worcester (full Karoo trim), then run the Tyre Pressure Calculator from the actual axle figures before the R355. Re-inflate for tar runs (R63, N1). Keep the slip — it closes the weighbridge action on return.
Pace
Slow on corrugation
The R355 and Tankwa interior are notoriously corrugated. Easy speeds save the vehicle and your fillings.
Recovery / spares
Self-reliant
Long stretches with no signal or traffic. Carry the full spares/tools kit; check the recovery gear before the remote legs.
Electrical shakedown
First field test
This loop is the first extended test of the Victron LiFePO4 stack. Log SmartShunt state-of-charge morning and evening; try solar-only days at Enjo and Verneukpan; test the 16A shore inlet at Melton Wold and Sutherland; note any BMS low-temp charge delay on frost mornings.

Watch the usual monitor items between legs — a quick under-vehicle look at each camp for the known seepage points, hubs and propshaft. The corrugated sections are exactly where small leaks show themselves. Add the daily battery state-of-charge reading to the same habit: 29 nights of real data is the energy budget the expedition planning has been missing. Everything logged here feeds the post-trip review on return.

Breakdown & Service

The single most useful fact about this route: there is no Toyota dealer anywhere in the middle of it. Nieuwoudtville, Loeriesfontein, Brandvlei, Vanwyksvlei and Carnarvon have no franchised Toyota presence, which means stops 2 through 5 — nine consecutive nights — sit several hundred kilometres from a dealer in any direction. That is not a reason to change the route; it is a reason to leave with the vehicle right and to know who to phone.

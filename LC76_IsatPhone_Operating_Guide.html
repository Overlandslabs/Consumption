This is the crew operating manual for the satellite phone. It is written so that either crew member can pick the phone up cold and use it — including in an emergency, with no cell coverage and no one to ask. The phone is a backup voice channel; the Garmin inReach is the tracking-and-SOS channel. In a serious emergency both run at the same time. Handset key-presses and menu paths in this guide are taken from the Inmarsat IsatPhone 2 User Guide (Model 2.1, September 2020) — the manual for this exact handset — with page references given so you can check the source. Emergency numbers here are a controlled copy of R29 §6; if a number here ever disagrees with R29 §6, R29 §6 is right and this page is wrong.

Two channels, two jobs — know which is whichThe inReach Mini 2 does tracking, GPS coordinates and one-button SOS; its SOS routes automatically to the Garmin emergency response centre (IERCC) — that path is built into the device. The IsatPhone does not have an SOS button that reaches a rescue centre — it is a telephone. It dials numbers. In an emergency you use the satphone to call our own medevac providers (Section 4). Two different paths, on purpose.

Coverage — does it work where we are going?

The IsatPhone works globally over the Inmarsat-4 satellites and Alphasat. [Guide p8] Alphasat coverage prioritises the region north of 44°S, and service may degrade south of that line. South Africa and everywhere on the planned Southern- and East-Africa route sit well north of 44°S, so the whole expedition corridor is inside strong coverage. The one thing to confirm before departure is that our specific Regional prepay plan (not just the satellite footprint) covers every country on the route — that is a pre-departure check, not a field one.

§2 Readiness Check Power · signal · registration

A satphone that is flat, or that has never seen the sky since you switched it on, is not a comms channel. Two habits keep it real: keep it charged, and power it up under open sky often enough to know it still registers. Do this on a schedule, not for the first time in a crisis.

Keeping it charged

The handset runs on a rechargeable lithium-ion battery. [Guide p11] Charge it from the house bank using the car charger (it accepts 10–30 V DC in), from mains, or from USB. [Guide p11, p71] For long storage between trips, Inmarsat advises keeping the battery around 80% charge, stored dry and cool, and checking it every three months, recharging if it drops below 40%. [Guide p60] Operating range is −20 °C to +55 °C; it only charges between 0 °C and +45 °C. [Guide p59] Do not leave it cooking on the dashboard.

Raising a signal — the drill

The phone talks to satellites above the equator, so the antenna must be up and there must be clear sky between the antenna and the satellite. [Guide p14] Trees, canopy, a vehicle roof, a gorge wall — any of these will block it.

Power-on to ready — every time
1
Step outside, into the open. If open sky is about 70% of what you see looking up, the signal should be strong. [p14]
2
Lift the antenna so it points upward, clear line of sight to the sky.
3
Hold the red key a few seconds until the screen lights and the Inmarsat logo shows. [p12]
4
Screen shows Searching satellite, then Ready for service, with Inmarsat at the top-left. [p14]
5
Check the signal bars. You need at least two bars to make or receive a call. [p14]
6
Check the GPS-fix icon is gone. The phone needs a GPS fix to be located by the satellite; it acquires one automatically — keep it in the open until the icon disappears. [p15]
If it will not registerIf it keeps showing Searching satellite, the on-screen eCompass points you toward the best satellite — turn the antenna the way it shows until the on-screen satellite goes green, then press Search. [p15] Move away from obstructions. Full troubleshooting is in §8.
Periodic readiness — not from the box, but on a clock

The phone is already configured, so this is a check, not a setup. Make it a standing habit: power up under open sky and confirm Ready for service and two bars; glance at the unit balance and expiry (§5); confirm the battery is charged. Do it monthly at home and again just before every trip. A phone that registered last month and is charged today is a channel you can trust; a phone you have not switched on since purchase is a hope, not a plan.

This is the working section: how to get around the menu, how to save a number so you never have to key a medevac line by hand in a crisis, and how to make and take calls and texts. It is an ordinary keypad phone — a few keys and one menu. If you ever get lost, the red key takes you back to the main screen. [Guide p7, p12]

Saving a number — adding a contact (do this before the trip)

There are two ways to save a number, and both take a few seconds. [Guide p41] The phone holds up to 500 contacts, the SIM up to 250. [p41]

Way 1 — you already have the number on screen
1
On the main screen, type the number in full international format (+.. — see "Dialling by hand" below).
2
Press Options > Add to contacts > Create new contact. [p41]
3
Choose the number type (Mobile, Office, etc.), then Options > Save.
Way 2 — straight into the phonebook
1
Menu > Contacts (the default store is Phonebook), then Options > New contact. [p41]
2
Enter the first name, last name and the telephone number (full international, +..).
3
For a second number or detail, Options > Add detail. When done, Options > Save (or the centre key).
Pre-load the medevac and family numbers — cold, before departureSave every number from R29 §6 (and home) into the phonebook before the trip, each in full international format. A number saved calmly at home is one you do not have to key correctly one-handed with a casualty on the ground. Save AMREF and ER24 first.
Calling or texting a saved contact

Menu > Contacts > Phonebook, then scroll with the navigation keys or type the first letters of the name to search. Highlight the contact and press the green key to call; if the contact has more than one number, pick it with the centre key. [Guide p16, p42] To text a saved contact: highlight it, Options > Send message > Text message. [p42] To return a recent call: Menu > Call log, pick Missed or Received, highlight, green key. [p17]

Speed dial — one key for a number you want fast

Speed dial is on by default. Slots 2–9 are yours; slot 1 is reserved for voicemail. [Guide p23] To assign one: Contacts > Phonebook, open the contact, highlight the number, Options > Assign speed dial, and pick a free slot. [p23] To dial: hold that number key for a couple of seconds and it calls. Worth setting one slot to the primary medevac line.

Phone vs SIM · checking our own number

Contacts can live in the phone's Phonebook or on the SIM; keeping a copy on both is a backup. [Guide p43] Copy phone→SIM: Phonebook > highlight > Options > Copy to SIM; SIM→phone: Menu > Contacts > SIM contacts > highlight > Options > Copy to phonebook. [p43] To check our own satellite number, open Info (centre key) > My phone number, or Menu > Contacts > My phone number. [p44]

Dialling a number by hand

The single most important rule on this phone: every number is dialled in full international format. A satellite handset has no "home network," so there is no local short-dial. Enter the number as + (hold the 0 key for 3 seconds to get the +) or 00, then the country code, then the area code without its leading 0, then the number, and press the green key. [Guide p16] For South Africa the country code is 27 — a Cape Town landline 021 123 4567 is dialled +27 21 123 4567. End a call with the red key.

Share-call and short-dial codes will NOT route by satelliteSA share-call numbers (starting 0860 / 0861) and short-dial codes (112 · 082 911 · 084 124) work only on an in-country mobile network. They cannot be dialled from the satphone, and putting +27 in front of a share-call number does not turn it into a dialable number. If a service you need only publishes a share-call line, you cannot reach it from here — use the full geographic (+27 …) alternative given in Section 4.
Taking a call

To receive a call the antenna must be up and the phone connected. Press the green key to answer, the red key to reject. [Guide p17] If the antenna is stowed or signal is marginal, the phone's Incoming call alerting will still flag an incoming call — you then have ~15 seconds to lift the antenna and get into the open to connect. [Guide p20]

Text messages (SMS)

Select Menu > Messaging > New message > Text message, type the message, add the recipient's full international number (or Options > Add recipient to pick a saved contact), then Options > Send. [Guide p29–30] Incoming voice and SMS are free; a text is cheaper than a call and gets through in weaker signal, so for non-urgent contact home, text rather than call. The phone can also send a text to an email address (Menu > Messaging > New message > Email message) — useful for reaching someone without a phone to hand. [Guide p30]

Voicemail

If voicemail is enabled, a new-voicemail icon appears; to listen, hold the 1 key, or dial short code 570, or the full number +870 772 001 899, then the green key, and follow the spoken prompts. [Guide p22]

§4 Emergency / SOS Who to call · what to say

In a medical or life-threatening emergency, the satphone's job is to call our own medevac providers — the people who will actually send help and whom we can claim against. There is no magic button that reaches a rescue centre from this handset; you dial. Run the inReach SOS and the satphone at the same time (they are independent channels). This section owns the numbers as a controlled copy; the drill and the call script are owned by R10 §9 — read that before departure, not with a casualty on the ground.

Call first. Argue cover later.Get the aircraft moving before you confirm who pays. A coverage question must never delay a clinical decision. Entitlement is recoverable after the fact; physiology is not. Make the call. (Full doctrine: R10 §9.)

Run both channels — the SOS drill (mirrors R29 §7, drill owned by R10 §9)
In a serious emergency
1
inReach SOS first. Hold the inReach SOS button 3 seconds, confirm on screen. Tracking starts and Garmin's response centre opens two-way text. Read your GPS coordinates off the inReach screen for the voice call.
2
Then call on the satphone. Get it to Ready for service (§2), and dial the right medevac number above in full international format.
3
Give the handover in fixed order — who / callback number / what happened / exact position / casualties & condition / what you request. The full five-item script is R10 §9. Read coordinates off the inReach; do not recite from memory.
4
Keep the inReach text channel open in parallel — it survives conditions that drop a voice call.
The Assistance button — optional, and it is not an SOS

The handset has a programmable Assistance button under a flip latch at the top. It is not the same as an emergency call: it rings, or texts, a recipient you pre-set — and by design it cannot dial a number shorter than four digits (so it can never be set to 112 or 082 911). [Guide p35] If configured, it would point at one of our own full-international provider or anchor numbers, send GPS with the alert, retry up to eight times, and give a 15-second cancel window before firing. [Guide p35–36]

Assistance-button target — decision pendingWhether to configure the Assistance button at all, and to which number, is an open owner decision (OI-3 / RA-10). Until it is set, treat §4 above — dial the provider yourself — as the emergency method. If it is later configured, its target and the "how to fire it" steps get added here.

§5 Prepay Units How many · how long · top-up

The phone runs on prepay units, and there are two clocks on it: how fast units burn when you talk, and when the bundle expires. For the Karoo trip the 20 units are plenty. As an expedition budget they are neither enough nor safe to leave sitting — the expiry is the real risk.

Check the balance and expiry — free, do it often

From the main screen enter the balance-enquiry code *106# and press the green key — the phone shows your current balance and expiry date on screen, free of charge. [Guide p34] You can also go Menu > Settings > Prepay > Balance enquiry. To load a voucher: *101*VoucherNumber# then the green key. [Guide p34] Make the balance check a standing habit — monthly and before every trip.

Balance-check code — verify which one appliesThe Inmarsat Model 2.1 user guide gives the balance code as *106# (above). Our purchase paperwork referenced 591 instead — some resellers route balance enquiries through their own short code. Confirm the correct code with Global Sat4Rent and, if it differs, correct this line. Either way the Menu > Settings > Prepay route always works.
The expiry — the thing most likely to catch us out

The current bundle expires 7 August 2027 — roughly ten months before a mid-2028 departure. Left alone, the units simply lapse, unused, before the expedition even starts. Topping up before the expiry date rolls the unused units forward, extends validity, and keeps the same number (+870 777 751 134). Let it lapse and you risk losing the units and the number. Put the expiry date somewhere you will see it.

Expedition airtime is a separate decisionThe 20-unit bundle is a domestic backup — right for the Karoo, where there is cell coverage almost everywhere. It is not an expedition airtime plan. Bundle size, validity and a top-up cadence for a two-year trip are decided closer to departure (~T-6 months) as a separate item — this guide flags it, it does not solve it.

§6 Field Doctrine Satphone vs inReach · care

Two satellite devices, and it matters which you reach for. The rule of thumb: the inReach is for reaching out with position and short text and for pressing SOS; the satphone is for a two-way conversation. When it is genuinely serious, you use both.

✓ Reach for the inReach when…
✓
You need SOS.
Its SOS button reaches the Garmin response centre automatically. The satphone has no such button.
✓
You want to send position or a short message.
Tracking and short texts survive weaker signal than a voice call.
✓
Battery or airtime is precious.
Texting sips power and units; a call gulps both.
→ Reach for the satphone when…
→
You need a real conversation.
Talking a coordinator through a casualty, or being talked through a fix — voice, not text.
→
You must reach a phone number, not a device.
A medevac desk, an insurer, family — the satphone dials them; the inReach cannot.
→
It is serious — then run BOTH.
inReach SOS for coordination and position, satphone for the voice call. Independent channels.
Line-of-sight discipline

Every call and message needs clear sky between the antenna and the satellite. [Guide p14, p19] Move out from under canopy, off the vehicle, away from a gorge wall or a building. If the signal drops mid-call, the eCompass shows which way to aim the antenna. [Guide p15] Keep the antenna pointed the way it shows when you bring the phone back to your ear.

Keep it charged · keep it dry

Keep the phone charged off the house bank so it is never the flat device in a crisis (§2). It is IP65 — dust- and splash-resistant, not waterproof — so keep the USB/audio cover closed and clean, and dry the handset if it gets wet before opening the cover. [Guide p59–60] Store it in the hardcase against knocks, dust and condensation, and let it acclimatise rather than charging a cold-soaked phone (it will not charge below 0 °C anyway). Take care folding the antenna — it can nip a fingertip. [Guide p5]

§8 Troubleshooting When it won't play

Most satphone problems are signal or power, and most are fixable in the field. Work down the list. [Guide p56–58]

Symptom → what to try
SymptomWhat to try
Won't switch onCheck the battery is seated; put it on charge — ~20 min gives enough to boot. If it will not charge at all, the battery is likely end-of-life. [p56]
Can't make / receive callsAntenna up, clear sky, ≥2 bars, valid GPS fix, sufficient battery. Rotate/angle the antenna to lift the bars. Check Call barring / Divert / Fixed-number-dialling aren't on. Check units (§5). [p56]
Stuck on "Searching satellite"Antenna fully up and pointed per the eCompass; move to clearer sky; confirm you are within coverage. [p57]
No GPS fix / GPS-fix icon staysKeep it in the open, screen up, until the icon clears; it re-tries every ~30 s. Some countries restrict GPS display — the phone still works, it just won't show the position. [p15, p39, p58]
Flat battery, nowhere to chargeCharge off the house bank via the car charger (10–30 V in). Send a short inReach text meanwhile. [p11, p71]
Balance / units questionDial *106# (or Menu > Settings > Prepay); if that code is rejected, the reseller code may differ — see §5. [p34]
Can't receive textsMessage memory may be full (max 20 in memory) — delete a few. If still nothing, the service-centre number may be wrong — contact the provider. [p57]
Screen in a language you can't readRed key → left key → down twice to Settings → open → down twice to Language → pick English. [p47, p57]

▌1. WHY THIS AUDIT EXISTS

Three audits have been run on this library. All three examined the
LIBRARY — reference documents, country profiles, coverage, currency,
technical accuracy. None examined the instrument that tracks them. The
register has been the auditor and has never been audited.

The gap is not a new discovery. It is written down in the Project
Instructions, in the roll-forward reconciliation convention adopted
24 July 2026:

    "LIMITATION, STATED SO THE GATE DOES NOT CREATE FALSE COMFORT:
     this catches SHAPE, NOT TRUTH. A well-formed entry containing an
     invented number passes every one of these assertions."

Every control built since tests shape. Numbering is sequential. Headers
balance. Next-free matches across two files. Closed items are not
deleted. All of it verifies entries are correctly FORMED. None asks
whether an entry is TRUE — whether it describes one job or three,
whether its source exists, whether "OPEN" is still the case, whether its
numbers are right, or whether anything would notice if it were wrong.

An entry can therefore be fictional, stale, arithmetically wrong, or
three jobs wearing one number, and pass every gate this library owns.


▌2. THE QUESTIONS THIS AUDIT MUST ANSWER

  Q1  WHAT IS OPEN, AND IN WHICH FILE DOES IT LIVE?
      Demands a defined POPULATION — every instrument capable of
      carrying an open status, established by sweep, not assumed.

  Q2  HOW DO YOU KNOW AN ITEM IS OPEN?
      Demands EVIDENCE for status. Today the answer is "because a line
      says OPEN" — self-declaration, unverified, no re-test date,
      nothing that would notice it going stale.

  Q3  WHAT ARE ALL THE SOURCES?
      Demands PROVENANCE. A source is a place a person can open, not a
      moment in a conversation that no longer exists.

  Q4  HOW DOES SOMETHING BECOME AN OPEN ITEM IN THE FIRST PLACE?
      Added on owner challenge. Completeness cannot be established by
      tracing between files — if something never enters the system, two
      instruments agree perfectly and both are wrong. See §4.

4.2  THE ACCEPTANCE FILTER

Not every proposal becomes an open item. Owner ruling: an item enters
the authoritative record only when the owner accepts that a gap exists
or a practice is not adhered to. Acceptance is an EVENT — it has a date
and it is recorded.

  · A proposal is a RECOMMENDATION until accepted. Recommendations and
    obligations must not be indistinguishable in the record; today
    roughly a third of entries trace to "Consultant review 8 Jul", which
    was me proposing.
  · DECLINED PROPOSALS ARE RECORDED — one line: proposal, date,
    declined, why. Not bureaucracy: without it the filter is untestable
    (a declined proposal and a lost one look identical), and this
    library has a documented history of re-litigating settled questions.
  · CUT-OFF AT THE GATE. Acceptance and recording happen in the same
    session, or the lag is itself flagged. The gap between "you agreed"
    and "it was written down" is where completeness actually fails.
  · NO BACK-DOOR CREATION. Items enter only through a defined stream.
    Nothing is typed straight into the register. This makes provenance a
    CONDITION OF BIRTH rather than something audited afterwards.

4.3  MATERIALITY — WHAT EARNS AN ITEM AT ALL

Materiality is consequence, not subject matter. A missing tyre lever and
a missing injector pipe both live in R3; only one ends the trip. Without
a threshold the register regrows past forty and becomes unusable, which
is how it arrived here.

AN ITEM IS WARRANTED IF ANY ONE HOLDS:
  M1  Safety or life-safety
  M2  Legal or regulatory — licence, carnet, insurance validity
  M3  Expedition-blocking — strands the vehicle or ends the trip
  M4  Irreversible or lead-time-bound — cannot be sourced or done en route
  M5  RETIRED — owner ruling D1, 30 Jul 2026. Cost is NOT a materiality
      test. This section opens "materiality is consequence, not subject
      matter", and a rand threshold was the one magnitude test in an
      otherwise consequence-based list — it contradicted the section it
      sat in. An action vital to safety, legality or mobility is warranted
      by M1–M4 / M6 regardless of price and is NEVER deferred, downgraded
      or de-warranted for cost. Where cost genuinely bears it is an INPUT
      to a DECISION item (grade of part, refurb vs new — OI-17 / OI-19 /
      OI-22), made by a recorded assessment weighing safety, service
      interval, sustainability and availability; it never sets a finding's
      rating. The former R50,000 working figure is WITHDRAWN and must not
      be re-attached.
  M6  Cross-document integrity — a fact other documents depend on is wrong

Everything else is a note in the owning document, NOT an item.
Applied: a spare with no African substitute is material, a convenience
tool is not; an unmitigated cascade link is material, the cascade
analysis is not; a seam with no owner is material — that is the failure
this project keeps repeating.

▌7. ASSERTIONS TESTED

Ten. The first five are the standard transaction assertions adapted to a
register of obligations; the last five are specific to this instrument.
An entry passing all ten is a TRUE entry.

  A1 OCCURRENCE AND RIGHTS
     The item records a real obligation, and it belongs to this project.
     TEST: the underlying condition exists and is re-observable. And: is
     this obligation OURS? One that is truly the workshop's, an
     insurer's or a supplier's is a dependency, not our action.
     FAILS IF: already done, overtaken, never real, or not ours to close.

  A2 COMPLETENESS
     Everything open appears, once.
     TEST: two-way. Every open row in every §6 instrument traces to a
     register entry; every entry traces back. Both directions — one-way
     tracing cannot see an omission. PLUS the §4 test: did every
     origination stream's capture point actually capture?
     FAILS IF: an obligation exists elsewhere with no entry, an entry
     duplicates another, or a stream leaks at the gate.

  A3 ACCURACY
     Every figure is right, and traceable to a record.
     TEST: each mileage, date, interval, cost, part number and reference
     traced to its source of record, and every DERIVED figure recomputed.
     "Overdue by X km" is an arithmetic claim with two inputs and a
     stated interval; all three are tested, not the result.
     FAILS IF: a figure cannot be traced, or does not recompute.
     WHY IT IS ITS OWN ASSERTION: a wrong number in a correctly formed
     entry passes every other test here and every gate the library owns.
     Two candidate exceptions are already visible — F-I.

  A4 CUT-OFF
     Items are recorded in the period the event occurred, and the
     register states the date it speaks as at.
     TEST: (i) work performed before a stated date is recorded against
     that date, not the date someone wrote it down; (ii) a closure dated
     in one session is not recorded in another; (iii) each entry carries
     an as-at date and the register states the moment its picture is
     true for; (iv) the acceptance-to-recording lag (§4.2).
     FAILS IF: an event is logged in the wrong period, or no as-at date
     exists — in which case 40 items are open as at a date nobody can
     name.

  A5 CLASSIFICATION
     Each item sits in the right place under the right label.
     TEST: correct section, correct type (§9), correct status value from
     the controlled vocabulary (§10), correct owner class, correct
     origination stream.
     FAILS IF: any is wrong or unassignable. An item whose type cannot
     be determined was usually never specified.

  A6 ATOMICITY — ONE ITEM, ONE JOB
     TEST: can it be closed by a single decision, purchase, visit or
     build? If closing requires two independent events, it is two items.
     FAILS IF: lettered sub-items close independently. Five entries fail
     today.

  A7 PROVENANCE
     The source names a place, not a moment.
     TEST: resolves to a file plus locator a person can open — document
     and section, sheet and row, a commit, a supplier reference, a
     physical artifact.
     FAILS IF: the source is a bare date, a session number, or the name
     of a conversation. 26 of 40 fail today.

  A8 STATUS VALIDITY AND CURRENCY
     TEST: status carries evidence at the grade §8 requires for its
     type, the date last confirmed, and the date next due for
     confirmation.
     FAILS IF: asserted without evidence, or last confirmed so long ago
     it cannot be relied on. 0 of 40 carry a re-confirmation date.

  A9 OWNERSHIP AND ACTIONABILITY
     TEST: owner is a person or role, not a project code; the entry
     states what closing it looks like.
     FAILS IF: ownership unclear, or the entry describes a worry rather
     than an action.

  A10 CLOSURE SUFFICIENCY (closed log)
     TEST: each closure names evidence meeting §8 for its type.
     FAILS IF: closed on narrative alone. [The "7 of 27 fail today"
     figure is SUPERSEDED — Session 3 found it unreproducible; see the
     F-D correction in §14. The STRICT reading is adopted as the closure
     standard (ruling D4): a closure names its commit AND a re-runnable
     validation; "validation clean" prose is E5.]


▌8. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Grades, strongest first. Required grade depends on item type (§9).
APPLIES AT ORIGINATION AND AT CLOSURE — issue 1 graded only closure,
which is why items born in conversation looked acceptable.

  E1  PHYSICAL / DOCUMENTARY ARTIFACT
      Weighbridge slip, invoice, licence card, photograph, test print,
      job card. Exists independently of this project and of me.
  E2  SYSTEM-VERIFIABLE MEASUREMENT
      A file at a pinned commit, an HTTP status, a spreadsheet row, a
      count from a script whose method is recorded and re-runnable.
  E3  THIRD-PARTY WRITTEN CONFIRMATION
      Supplier email, provider reference, manual citation. Already done
      correctly once: WCG Contact Centre ref 260717-000921.
  E4  RECORDED OWNER RULING
      Dated decision written into a governance file. For DECISION items
      this is primary evidence — a decision has no other artifact. For
      physical items it is not evidence; it is an instruction.
  E5  ASSISTANT NARRATIVE OR RECOLLECTION
      A session block, a chat, a summary, my account of what I did.

  THE RULE: E5 IS NOT EVIDENCE. It may point at evidence, and today it
  is often all that exists. Where an item rests on E5 that is a finding,
  and remediation is to obtain E1–E4 or state plainly that none exists.

  AND IT APPLIES TO ME: my report that I did something is E5.
  "Validation clean, depth walks 0/0/0" is E2 only if the script and its
  output are recorded and re-runnable. Otherwise it is me saying so.


▌9. ITEM TYPOLOGY AND CLOSURE TESTS

  T1 PHYSICAL WORK      closes on invoice, job card, or dated photo · E1
  T2 PURCHASE           closes on invoice or proof of possession · E1
  T3 MEASUREMENT        closes on the recorded measurement, with date,
                        method and who took it · E1/E2
  T4 DECISION           closes on a dated owner ruling in the file that
                        owns the topic, plus its propagation sweep · E4
  T5 BUILD              closes on the file at a named commit with its
                        validation output recorded · E2
  T6 EXTERNAL DEPENDENCY closes on third-party written confirmation · E3
  T7 STANDING           NEVER CLOSES. Carries a cadence and a last-
                        performed date. A standing item showing open
                        forever is not a failure; one with no last-
                        performed date is.


▌10. CONTROLLED STATUS VOCABULARY

Status is currently free text — the working papers alone carry more than
twenty distinct strings across three sheets, including a full sentence
used as a status value. Free-text status cannot be counted or trusted.

  OPEN                   live, actionable now
  BLOCKED                cannot proceed; the blocker is named
  WAITING-DATE           held by a trigger date or event, which is stated
  DONE-PENDING-EVIDENCE  work performed, evidence not yet filed
  CLOSED                 closed with evidence at the required grade
  SUPERSEDED             overtaken; the superseding item is named
  STANDING               recurring; carries a cadence

DONE-PENDING-EVIDENCE is deliberate. It is the honest state for much of
what currently reads as closed, and naming it prevents both failure
modes: pretending finished work is unfinished, and pretending asserted
work is verified.


▌11. THE REGISTER AS DATABASE — SCHEMA AND VALIDATOR

Owner ruling: the .txt register is master (§5). The reasoning is sound
and stronger than the spreadsheet alternative first proposed —

  · A .xlsx is a binary blob. It cannot be diffed. This entire working
    method rests on SHA-pinning and reading what changed between
    commits; a spreadsheet master makes every change to the
    authoritative record invisible to review.
  · Plain text diffs, greps, survives any device, and holds reasoning
    and annotation that a cell destroys.
  · The field-discipline objection died on 29 July 2026: a parser read
    all 40 entries into structured data with full fidelity. A TEXT FILE
    WITH AN ENFORCED SCHEMA IS A DATABASE. It is also readable.

THE CONDITION, AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL: a schema is only real if
something rejects violations. A shape convention was written on the
morning of 29 July and five entries still had to be repaired that
afternoon — the ownership line buried in a wrapped title, invisible to
the eye, fatal to a parser. A declared format with no validator is a
wish.

  R1 FIELDS, fixed order, with controlled vocabulary on status, type and
     evidence grade. Plain-language line mandatory (adopted 29 Jul).
  R2 A VALIDATOR RUN EVERY SESSION that parses all entries and fails
     loudly on any non-conforming one. Same machinery as the parser,
     pointed at the file as a gate rather than as a reader.
  R3 THE MASTERSHIP MAP (§5) enforced — the register is master of
     STATUS, not of everything, or the duplication problem is rebuilt
     inside the solution.


▌12. METHOD

  M1 100% COVERAGE, NOT SAMPLING. Forty items is small enough to test
     exhaustively, and sampling assumes a homogeneous population. These
     entries were written across four weeks under varying discipline.
  M2 ONE FINDING SHEET PER ITEM — item, type, stream, ten assertions
     pass/fail, evidence found, evidence required, remediation, rating.
  M3 EXCEPTION RATING. Rating is by CONSEQUENCE OF THE REGISTER ERROR
     alone; cost and quantum are NEVER rating inputs (owner ruling D1/D2,
     30 Jul 2026).
       SIGNIFICANT — could cause the wrong thing to be done, or a real
                     obligation to be missed. Safety items significant
                     by default.
       MODERATE    — entry unreliable, underlying job not at risk.
       HOUSEKEEPING— form only.
  M4 NO EDITS DURING FIELDWORK. Findings first, complete, presented as
     one document; the owner rules; remediation is a separate pass.
     Remediating while testing destroys the evidence of what was wrong —
     and the sessions of 29 July showed what happens when building runs
     ahead of checking.
  M5 TWO-WAY TRACE, recorded as a matrix.
  M6 EVERY MEASUREMENT RE-RUNNABLE, naming the script that produced it.
     A number I cannot reproduce on demand is E5.
  M7 SUSPECT THE PROBE FIRST. Seven probe errors across two sessions,
     all false, all caught. A probe disagreeing with a file is wrong
     until proven otherwise.

  S5 THE AUDIT IS ITSELF AUDITABLE — working papers survive the session.

▌15. WHAT THE DASHBOARD MUST SHOW AFTERWARDS

The output is not a report. It is a register worth displaying, and a
display that shows its own reliability. Each item carries:

  · the plain-English job                    (built; needs owner review)
  · its type, and therefore what closes it                    (§9)
  · its origination stream                                  (§4.1)
  · its source — file and locator, openable                    (A7)
  · the evidence grade behind its status                       (§8)
  · when status was last confirmed, and when next              (A8)
  · its as-at date                                             (A4)
  · who must move it                                           (A9)

An item resting on E5 displays as such. A register that shows where it
is weak is more use than one that looks uniformly confident, and it is
the only version that can be trusted in the field.


▌16. LIMITATIONS

  L1 This audit tests the RECORD, not the WORLD. It can establish that
     an item claims a hub seal is weeping and that the claim traces to a
     workshop advisory. It cannot establish that the seal is weeping.
  L2 It cannot recover evidence that never existed. Where an item rests
     on a conversation, the honest output is "no artifact exists", plus
     a decision to create one or drop the item.
  L3 It does not fix the library's content. That remains EX-10 and the
     content-audit programme.
  L4 It is performed by the party that wrote most of the entries. §13
     reduces the threat; it does not remove it.

APPENDIX A — SYSTEMIC ITEM GENERATORS (stream S5)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Chartered as Session 7. Four generators already exist in the library
(R41 cascades, R41 seams, R38 §6 assumptions, audit F-series). Eight
further generators follow, each with a live candidate in this project.
These produce the items nobody thought to raise — which is precisely why
they are the ones that bite.

  G1  COMMON-MODE FAILURE — redundancy that isn't.
      Two independent-looking safeguards sharing a hidden dependency.
      LIVE CANDIDATE: the inReach and the planned IsatPhone sit on two
      different satellite constellations and BOTH ROUTE SOS TO THE SAME
      GARMIN COORDINATION CENTRE. Independent at the handset,
      single-threaded at the receiving end. The same question applies to
      two batteries served by one alternator.

  G2  SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE WITH NO FIELD SUBSTITUTE.
      Distinct from a cascade: not what follows a failure, but what has
      no second path. One spare tyre north of the Zambezi is already an
      item; the system has never been swept for the others.

  G3  EXPIRY MAPPING AGAINST THE TRIP TIMELINE.
      Every dated validity plotted against 2028–2030: licence cards,
      disc, the affidavit's ~3-month life, the carnet's 12 months,
      insurance duration caps, tyre DOT dates, first-aid certification,
      medication. Anything expiring mid-trip is an item.
      NOTE: Mary-Ann's card expiry was found this way — BY ACCIDENT.
      Done systematically, one pass catches the rest.

  G4  CONSTRAINT INTERLOCKS.
      Where two constraints are individually satisfiable and jointly
      impossible. The carnet's 12 months against a 24-month no-return
      trip is exactly this, and is the top open constraint. The other
      constraints have never been tested pairwise.

  G5  SENSING GAPS — what changes with no instrument watching.
      A stock that drains invisibly. What would tell you the house
      battery is degrading, or that a seal has started weeping, BETWEEN
      services? Where there is no sensor there is drift, and drift is
      discovered late. This generator produced OI-59 itself: the
      register was unusable and nothing reported that.

  G6  LIMIT PROXIMITY.
      Anything running near a hard boundary. Payload against a 3,000 kg
      GVM, where one estimate already sits ~50 kg over. Alternator
      capacity against DC-DC draw. Water budget against stay length. A
      margin below threshold is an item by definition.

  G7  SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS OF OUR OWN MODIFICATIONS.
      Every change creates obligations that did not previously exist.
      The turbo brought EGT monitoring. LiFePO4 brought low-temperature
      charge cutoff. What did the free-flow exhaust bring? Nobody has
      asked.

  G8  CAPABILITY CONCENTRATION.
      Tasks only one person can perform. RA-8 treats this for Mary-Ann
      as a single readiness action rather than a systematic sweep.
      IT APPLIES TO THE ASSISTANT TOO: this project holds knowledge
      existing only in session narrative, which is the same failure
      shape as a one-person capability.

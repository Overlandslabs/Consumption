SESSION ORIGIN — AN EXTERNAL VIDEO USED AS AN AUDIT INSTRUMENT. The owner
brought an Overland Workshop (Aspyr) video on electrical-system design
principles and asked how it sat against the library. I cannot watch video;
the owner supplied the transcript, which is the correct division of
labour and worth repeating. The video's central claim — that a lithium
bank connected to an alternator WITHOUT a DC-DC charger can draw enough
current to burn the alternator out — independently corroborates the
rationale for the Orion ~40 A cap (R7/R26). Graded V. On the 1HZ, whose
alternator is smaller than the 120–180 A European units the presenter
cites, the cap matters MORE, not less. His alternator-upgrade suggestion
is a path R31 already rejected and RA-4 ruled on (24 Jul, no spare
carried). His solar pessimism is NOT imported: it comes from a 400 Ah
mission with Starlink and an electric oven, which is not ours.

WHAT THE VIDEO ACTUALLY EXPOSED was not a doctrine error but a GAP: the
library modelled a single day well and a multi-day STAY not at all. R7's
"~2.2 days wild-camp autonomy" is a ZERO-INPUT floor — it ignores solar
entirely — so it badly understates a real stationary stay with the
blanket out, and it was the only multi-day number in the library. The
owner's question ("if we stay 3 days, or 5, what do we need to do?") had
no tool that answered it.

MULTI-DAY STAY BLOCK BUILT (LiFePO4 Calculator). Four inputs — days at
stop 1–14 · arrival SOC · sun scenario (good 5.5 h / average 4.0 h /
poor 2.5 h peak-sun-hours) · recharge-drive length (default 3 h). Outputs
— Energy In / Out / Net triptych, a day-by-day SOC projection table with
green/amber/red banding, and a verdict naming how many recharge drives a
stay needs and on which days. OWNER-RATIFIED MODEL: stationary days are
solar-only (engine off, so no alternator term); a recharge drive fires
ONLY on a day the bank would otherwise breach the 20 % floor — the
owner's "drive only when needed" doctrine ("we don't mind a 3-hour drive
when we camp and explore"), NOT a fixed every-N-days cadence. The SOC
trajectory carries no loss factor, matching the existing single-day
maths so the two cannot disagree.

DAILY-LOAD FIGURE SUPERSEDED — 77 → 65.3 Ah/day (OI-48, opened and closed
same day). Found by SIMULATING the new block against the calculator's own
lc76 load preset, which summed to 65.3 while R7's headline said 77 and
R7's own autonomy figure implied ~69. Three numbers for one fact. THE
DIAGNOSIS IS THE INTERESTING PART: the breakdowns were right and the
headline was wrong. R5's itemised table AND the calculator's reference-
load callout both already summed to 65.3 — the "77" total sat on top of
correct component lists in two separate files and had never been
reconciled to them. OWNER RULING: 65.3 authoritative, no Starlink
carried. All corrections move in the FAVOURABLE direction — longer
autonomy, lower DoD, break-even solar in full sun, longer battery life.

TWO MODEL ARTIFACTS DELIBERATELY NOT RE-DERIVED (owner-agreed), because
recomputing them would have meant inventing numbers: (a) R7's capacity-
fade curve was modelled at the higher DoD, so at 31 % it is now
CONSERVATIVE — real retention should meet or exceed it. A basis note
saying exactly that was added to R7; the curve was NOT recomputed,
because a replacement needs Enertec DoD-vs-cycle-life data and an
invented curve reads as authority it has not earned (F65 class).
(b) R5's day-by-day autonomy table runs on a deliberately conservative
~41 Ah/day net (shade, dust, winter sun) DECOUPLED from the headline
load, so it stays valid; a one-line pointer to the Multi-day Stay block
was added so a reader does not mistake the two readings for a
contradiction.

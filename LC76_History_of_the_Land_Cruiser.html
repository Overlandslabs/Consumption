Go anywhere — and come home.

§ Why This History Matters

Most vehicles are designed to be replaced. The Land Cruiser was designed to be relied upon, and that difference has shaped seventy-five years of its history. It is Toyota's longest-running model line [TUK-HD], and by Toyota's own count, given at the launch of the newest model in October 2025, about 12.15 million have been sold in more than 190 countries and regions [T-FJ25]. A South African review from 2026 puts the figure at "over 12.4 million" [CARS-FJ]; the Toyota figure is used here because it carries a date and an owner.

Toyota describes the purpose of the vehicle in plain terms: it must be able to go anywhere, and it must bring the people inside it back safely [T-FJ25]. That is not a marketing line so much as an engineering brief, and it is the reason this history sits in the Philosophy section of the library rather than among the workshop documents. Our Manifesto asks for simplicity, reliability and "eco not ego". The Land Cruiser story is, for long stretches, a story of a manufacturer choosing exactly those things — keeping an old, simple design in production for the people who depend on it, long after the showroom has moved on.

This document is for reading and learning, not for the roadside. It covers every market and every branch of the family, up to the newest models. It names no prices. Every figure carries a short source key in square brackets, such as [T-HD]; the keys are listed in full in the last section, together with a table of the places where the sources disagree. Where they disagree, both versions are shown. Nothing has been chosen quietly.

The family tree, in Toyota's own words · [TT-70] [W-LC] HEAVY DUTY — the working line: the 40 Series, then the 70 Series STATION WAGON — the big family wagons: 55, 60, 80, 100, 200, 300 LIGHT DUTY — the lighter wagons that became the Prado: 70 wagon, 90, 120, 150, 250 LAND CRUISER FJ — a fourth, compact branch, added in 2025–26

Toyota itself describes the family as three branches, all descended from the original 1951 vehicle [TT-70], and a fourth, compact branch — the Land Cruiser FJ — arrived in 2025–26, below the Prado [W-LC]. A word on the numbers: Toyota names each generation by a "series" number — 40, 70, 80 and so on. The number is a family name, not a size or a year. How the full model codes are built is explained in the section Reading a Model Code.

Origins — The BJ and the 20 Series 1951 – 1960

The Land Cruiser began as a government order. During the Korean War, Japan's National Police Reserve wanted about a thousand Jeep-type four-wheel-drive vehicles, built from Japanese materials [TUK-HD]. Toyota's answer, a prototype finished in January 1951, was assembled largely from parts it already had: the chassis of its Type SB one-ton truck, its Type B 3.4-litre six-cylinder petrol engine, and suspension from a Toyota passenger car [TUK-HD]. The name followed the recipe. "BJ" meant the B engine plus "Jeep" [TUK-HD].

In 1951 the BJ became the first vehicle to climb to the sixth station of Mount Fuji [T-FJ25][TUK-HD]. The sources do not agree on the month — Toyota UK and others say July, Red Line Land Cruisers says August [TUK-HD][CARBUZZ][RLLC] — so this document says only "1951". One secondary account names the driver as Ichiro Taira; the Toyota pages checked for this document do not name him, so the name is reported only.

The climb did not, on its own, win the police order. The Reserve first chose Mitsubishi's licence-built Willys Jeep [T-HD]; Toyota UK says a further trial then won Toyota the supply contract [TUK-HD]. Volume production began in 1953 [T-HD], with series production from August 1953 [TUK-HD]. Because "Jeep" was a trademark of Willys-Overland, Toyota renamed the vehicle the Land Cruiser in June 1954 [T-HD]. (The Land Cruiser Heritage Museum gives the renaming as 1955 [LCHM]; this document follows Toyota.) The first exports went to Pakistan in 1954 and to Saudi Arabia in 1955 [TUK-HD].

The 20 Series followed — November 1955 according to Toyota Japan [T-HD], August 1955 according to Toyota UK [TUK-HD]. Its body was redesigned for civilian owners [T-HD]. It came on two wheelbases, 2,285 mm and 2,430 mm, with a third of 2,650 mm added in 1959 [T-HD]. (The wheelbase is the distance between the front and rear axles; a longer wheelbase means a longer body.) Engines were the 3.4-litre Type B and the 3.9-litre Type F petrol sixes, and from mid-1956 the Type F only [T-HD]. Toyota UK calls the F Toyota's longest-serving engine, still in use until late 1992 [TUK-HD].

The 20 Series is where the Land Cruiser went abroad in earnest. Toyota dates the start of volume exports to North America and elsewhere to this model [T-HD]. In 1957 Land Cruisers made up 38.2% of all vehicles exported from Japan [TUK-HD], and in many countries the Land Cruiser was the first Toyota ever sold [TUK-HD]. It was also the vehicle Toyota first built outside Japan: Toyota Times, Toyota's own magazine, records that Toyota do Brasil was set up in 1958 and that in May of the following year the Bandeirante, "Toyota's first overseas production vehicle", was born [TT-BR]. That story continues under the 40 Series and in Around the World.

The Workhorses — 40 and 70 Series 1960 – today

The 40 Series arrived in August 1960 [T-HD] and became the shape most people still picture when they hear the name. It was built on the 2,285, 2,430 and 2,650 mm wheelbases, with a 2,950 mm version added in 1963 for export pickups and cab-chassis models [T-HD]. Its most important first was mechanical: it was the first Land Cruiser with a two-speed transfer case, which gives the driver a separate set of very low gears — "low range" — for steep, slow or rough ground [W-J40][TOY-US]. The 20 Series had relied on a very low first gear instead [TOY-US].

The 40 started on the F petrol six [T-HD]. Diesel followed: the H, a 3.6-litre six-cylinder diesel, in 1972–73 (Toyota Japan says 1973 [T-HD], Toyota UK 1972 [TUK-HD]), then the B, a 3.0-litre four-cylinder diesel, in 1974 [T-HD]. The 2F 4.2-litre petrol and a four-speed gearbox came in 1975 [TOY-US]. In Japan, diesels became the popular choice because they were taxed more lightly [T-HD]. In West Africa the long troop-carrier body earned the nickname "Bush Taxi" [TUK-HD], and in 1980 the millionth Land Cruiser was built [TUK-HD][TOY-US].

Japanese production of the 40 ended in the mid-1980s — 1984 according to Wikipedia and Toyota USA [W-J40][TOY-US], 1986 according to Toyota UK [TUK-HD]. But the design lived on in Brazil. The Bandeirante carried the 40's shape until 2001 [TUK-HD][W-J40], fitted first with Mercedes-Benz diesels and from 1994 with Toyota's own 14B diesel [W-J40].

The 70 Series replaced the 40 in November 1984 [T-HD] and is still in production. It is the branch our own vehicle belongs to. Toyota built it on five wheelbases, from 2,310 to 3,180 mm [T-HD], with soft-top, van and fibreglass-roof bodies, and as a bare chassis for customers to build their own bodywork on [T-HD]. The two-digit number tells you the wheelbase: 70 and 71 are the short ones, 73 and 74 medium, 75, 78 and 79 long, while 77 and 76 are semi-long four-door wagons sold in a few markets [W-J70].

The 70 changed slowly, and on purpose. In 1999 the front leaf springs gave way to coil springs [T-HD]; from that year the Troop Carrier is the 78 and the pickup the 79 [W-J70]. In 2004 Japanese sales ended and the 70 became an overseas-only model [T-HD]. The 2007 facelift was its first major redesign: a new front end, with the front widened by 80 mm and the engine bay by 230 mm to make room for a new V8 turbo diesel [T-HD]. In the same change, Toyota added the 76 Series four-door semi-long wagon and dropped the 73 medium-wheelbase model [T-HD]. Twin airbags and a redesigned dashboard followed in 2009 [TUK-HD], and a double-cab pickup in August 2012 [T-HD].

Japan could not quite let it go. In 2014, for its thirtieth anniversary, Toyota re-released the 70 in Japan for about a year with the 4.0-litre V6 petrol [T-HD][T-70-30]. In November 2023 it came back to Japan as a regular model, now with the 1GD-FTV 2.8-litre turbo diesel — 150 kW (204 PS) and 500 Nm — and a six-speed automatic [T-70JP]. (A kilowatt, kW, measures power; PS is the metric horsepower Toyota Japan uses; a newton-metre, Nm, measures torque — the twisting force that pulls a heavy vehicle up a hill.)

The same 2.8-litre four-cylinder reached Australia and South Africa in the 2023–24 update, at 150 kW and 500 Nm with the automatic [CD-AU][CARS-70]; a five-speed manual version followed in Australia, rated at 450 Nm [CE-AU]. The V8 then bowed out. In Australia, V8 production ended in September 2024, apart from the 79 GXL utes, whose deliveries finished in the last quarter of 2025 [T-AU][CE-AU]. In South Africa the V8 was dropped in the 2026 update [T-ZA26], leaving a range of the 4.0 V6 petrol, the 4.2 diesel — the 1HZ, at 96 kW and 285 Nm — and the 2.8 GD-6 [T-ZA26][CARMAG].

Over its life the 70 has used four-, five- and six-cylinder engines, petrol and diesel, plus a V8 diesel for export, in Toyota's own summary [T-HD]; a 2024 Russian-language feature puts the total at about fifteen engines [IDA]. The engine section below lists them with their years.

The Station Wagons — 55 to 300 1967 – today

The wagon branch began as a sideline and became the flagship. The 55 Series, launched in August 1967 on a 2,700 mm wheelbase, replaced the FJ45V four-door van [T-SW]. In Japan it was classed as a commercial van; overseas it was sold as a passenger car [T-SW]. It used the F petrol six at 125 hp (130 hp from 1969), a three-speed manual gearbox and a two-speed transfer case [T-SW], and in Japan the 4,230 cc 2F from January 1975 [T-SW]. It was the first true Land Cruiser station wagon, and Toyota UK says it grew to about 11% of Land Cruiser sales [TUK-SW].

The 60 Series (August 1980 [T-SW]) brought comfort into the family: air conditioning, power steering and fabric seats all became available [T-SW]. It launched with the 2F 4.2 petrol and a 3.4 diesel, added the 2H 3,980 cc diesel six two years later, and gained turbocharged and fuel-injected versions later in its life [T-SW]. The best remembered is the 12H-T, a turbocharged, direct-injection version of the 2H — the "whistling" HJ61 [TUK-SW][W-H]. (A turbocharger uses exhaust gas to force more air into the engine; "direct injection" means the fuel is sprayed straight into the cylinder rather than into a small pre-chamber beside it.) A four-speed automatic arrived in 1984, which Toyota UK calls the first automatic in a Japanese four-wheel drive [TUK-SW]. From August 1986 the four-wheel drive and the differential locks were engaged with push-buttons that worked the mechanism by air pressure [TUK-SW]. The last 60 was built in December 1989 after a run of 406,700, and the model was also assembled in Venezuela [TUK-SW].

The 80 Series was launched in October 1989 in Japan [T-SW]; Toyota UK dates it to 1990, when the export markets received it [TUK-SW]. At 4,970 mm long and 1,930 mm wide it was deliberately moved up to luxury-vehicle level [T-SW]. Coil springs replaced leaf springs all round, while the solid axles were kept [T-SW][TUK-SW] — a solid axle is a single rigid beam joining the two wheels, simple and very strong, the traditional choice for rough ground. Higher models had full-time four-wheel drive with a centre differential [T-SW]. Engines included the 3F-E petrol at launch, the naturally aspirated (no turbo) 1HZ 4.2 diesel and the 1HD-T turbo diesel [T-SW][CG-80], the 1FZ-FE 4.5 petrol six from August 1992 at 215 PS [T-SW], and the 24-valve 1HD-FT turbo diesel from 1995 [TUK-SW][W-HD].

The 80 is widely regarded as the last full-size Land Cruiser wagon with a solid front axle [JDM-80] — an enthusiast view, borne out by the 100 Series' change to independent front suspension. In Colombia and Venezuela it was nicknamed "Burbuja", the bubble, and sold as the "Autana" [FAN]. Japanese production ended in 1997 [TUK-SW], but assembly from kits continued at Cumaná in Venezuela: the Land Cruiser Heritage Museum holds a 2007-model Autana assembled in November 2006, one of the last [LCHM-80]. The 80 also gave Lexus its first LX, the LX 450 of 1996 [W-LX].

The 100 Series (January 1998 [T-SW]) brought the biggest change in the wagon line's history. Its new 2UZ-FE 4.7-litre V8 petrol, at 235 PS, was the first V8 in a Land Cruiser [T-SW][TUK-SW], and a 1HD-FTE 4.2 turbo diesel went into the van model [T-SW]. At the front, a solid axle gave way to independent front suspension — each front wheel on its own arms, here double wishbones sprung by torsion bars [T-SW] — which rides and steers better on the road but is less simple in the bush. Top models had height control and electronically adjusted damping; the Cygnus was the top grade in Japan from December 1998 [T-SW].

For Africa, Australia and the Middle East, Toyota kept a tougher version: the 105, which put the 100 body on a solid front axle [CG-105]. It was offered with the 1HZ diesel or the 1FZ-FE petrol, not with the 1HD-FTE or the V8 [CG-105] — Toyota UK says Toyota kept a solid-axle version for loyal customers, but not with the big turbo diesel [TUK-SW]. One secondary source reports a GXL 105 with the 1HD-FTE in some markets [MOD]; an Australian buyer's guide checked for this document lists only the 1HZ and the petrol six across all 105 grades, including the GXL [DRIVA], so that claim stays reported. The Lexus version of the 100 was the LX 470 [W-LX].

The 200 Series was launched in September 2007 [T-SW], reaching Toyota UK's markets in early 2008 [TUK-SW], and was sold in more than 100 countries [T-SW]. Toyota called its Crawl Control — an off-road system that holds a slow, steady speed so the driver only has to steer — a world first, and it used a Torsen limited-slip centre differential [T-SW]. Its engines were the 2UZ-FE V8 petrol, now with variable valve timing [T-SW]; the 5.7-litre 3UR-FE petrol in North America [T-SW]; the 1UR-FE 4.6 petrol from about 2012 [LCL]; and, most significantly, the 1VD-FTV 4.5-litre twin-turbo V8 diesel, Toyota's first V8 diesel, developed in January 2007 [T-ENG][W-VD]. Its torque is given as 650 Nm [W-VD]; its power is quoted differently by different sources, at about 200 kW depending on market (see the conflicts table). The Lexus version was the LX 570 [W-LX]. After the 2021 model year the Land Cruiser name left the United States [EDM].

The 300 Series had its world premiere on 10 June 2021, the Land Cruiser's seventieth anniversary [TT-70], and went on sale on 2 August 2021 [T-300]. It sits on the GA-F platform, Toyota's first new-architecture platform for a body-on-frame vehicle — one built the traditional way, with the body bolted onto a separate ladder frame [TEU-300]. The diesel is the F33A-FTV 3.3-litre twin-turbo V6, at 227 kW (309 PS) and 700 Nm [T-300], Toyota's first V6 diesel [DRIVE]; the petrol is the V35A-FTS 3.5 twin-turbo V6 at 415 PS and 650 Nm [PT-300]. Both drive through a ten-speed automatic [TEU-300]. Toyota calls its E-KDSS — an electronically controlled system that frees the anti-roll bars so the wheels can drop further over rough ground — a world first, and its Multi-Terrain Monitor camera system a first for any Toyota [TEU-300].

A hybrid followed in 2025–26: the V35A petrol with a 36 kW, 250 Nm electric motor and the ten-speed automatic, for a combined 341 kW and 790 Nm [CC-300H], marketed as "457" horsepower in the United Arab Emirates [CC-300H][CS-300H]. It went on sale in eastern Europe from January 2026 [TEU-300H] and in Australia during 2026 [CC-300H]. The Lexus versions are the LX 600 and 500d [W-F33], and the 300 is not sold as a Toyota in the United States [EDM].

The Prado — A Side Branch 1985 – today

The Light Duty branch is a side road in this history, but a busy one. It began in October 1985 as the Land Cruiser Wagon, built on the short 70 van but softened with coil springs and powered by the 2L-T 2.4-litre turbo diesel [T-LD]. Some markets called it the Bundera or Land Cruiser II [TUK-HD]. In April 1990 it was split off and named the Land Cruiser Prado, aimed squarely at the Mitsubishi Pajero, with three- and five-door bodies and part-time four-wheel drive [T-LD]. Its diesels were the 2L-TE at 97 PS and, from May 1993, a 3.0-litre 130 PS turbo diesel, the 1KZ [T-LD].

The Prado 90 (May 1996) moved to independent front suspension and full-time four-wheel drive [T-LD]. It shared its underpinnings with the Hilux Surf, and was sold in the United Kingdom as the Colorado [T-LD][TUK-HD]. Its engines included the 5VZ-FE 3.4 V6 petrol, an intercooled 1KZ-TE at 140 PS, the 3RZ-FE 2.7 petrol from 1997, and from July 2000 the 1KD-FTV 3.0 common-rail diesel [T-LD]. (An intercooler cools the air coming from the turbocharger so the engine can burn more fuel cleanly. A common-rail diesel keeps its fuel in a single high-pressure pipe feeding electronically controlled injectors, instead of a mechanical pump timing each squirt.) Toyota UK calls the 1KD-FTV "Toyota's first common rail diesel", but Toyota's own 75-year history gives that title to the 1CD-FTV of 1999 [TUK-HD][T-ENG]; the safe statement is that the 1KD-FTV was the first common-rail diesel in a Land Cruiser.

The Prado 120 (October 2002) added a Torsen centre differential, electronic traction control and rear air suspension [T-LD]. It was styled by Toyota's ED2 design studio in France and formed the basis of the FJ Cruiser from 2006 [TUK-HD]; its Lexus twin was the GX 470 [W-GX]. The Prado 150 (September 2009) brought Crawl Control and Multi-Terrain Select [T-LD], and from 2015 the 1GD-FTV 2.8 diesel — 450 Nm with the automatic, uprated to 150 kW and 500 Nm in October 2020 [TUK-HD][W-GD]. Its Lexus twin was the GX 460 [W-GX].

The newest Prado, the 250, had its world premiere in August 2023 and reached Japan in April 2024 [T-250][PT-250J]. Toyota describes it as a "back to basics" Light Duty model [T-250][PT-250]. It shares the 300's GA-F platform and its 2,850 mm wheelbase [PT-250J]. Engines vary by market: the 1GD-FTV 2.8 diesel at 204 PS and 500 Nm with an eight-speed automatic (with a 48-volt mild-hybrid assist in Australia and Western Europe); a 2.4 turbo petrol hybrid, sold in the United States as the "i-FORCE MAX" at 326 hp; and the 2TR-FE 2.7 petrol [PT-250]. In the United States it is simply called the "Land Cruiser", from the 2024 model year [TS-US]. Its Lexus twin is the GX 550 [W-GX].

The Land Cruiser FJ — The Newest Branch 2025 –

The Land Cruiser FJ was revealed in October 2025 [T-FJ25] and launched in 2026: in Thailand at the end of March, in South Africa on 10 May and in Japan on 14 May [PT-FJ][W-FJ]. It is built by Toyota Motor Thailand [W-FJ] on a shortened version of the IMV ladder frame that also carries the Hilux and Fortuner, with a 2,580 mm wheelbase [W-FJ][PM-FJ]. It uses the 2TR-FE 2.7-litre petrol with a six-speed automatic, and part-time four-wheel drive with low range and a locking rear differential [PM-FJ] — a locking differential forces both wheels on an axle to turn together, so a wheel in the air cannot steal all the drive.

Its output depends on where it is sold: 163 PS and 246 Nm in Japan [PM-FJ], 166 PS and 245 Nm in Thailand [PT-FJ], 122 kW and 245 Nm in South Africa [CARS-FJ]. A 2.8 diesel has been rumoured but is unconfirmed [CIT-FJ]. The FJ is a small vehicle carrying a large name back to its roots: short, simple, ladder-framed, with low range — much what the 40 Series was.

The Engines — Families, and Where They Went

Toyota names its engines with a short code. The number in front is the version within a family (1HZ, 2H); the letters are the family itself (HZ, HD, VD); and the letters after the dash describe the build. Among the engines in this story, T marks a turbocharger (1HD-T), E electronic fuel control (1HD-FTE), F appears on the multi-valve engines (1HD-FT, 1FZ-FE), and V on the common-rail diesels (1KD-FTV, 1VD-FTV, 1GD-FTV). Toyota's full suffix scheme has more letters than are needed here. Where the letters appear inside a model code — the HZ in HZJ76 — they name the engine the vehicle was built with; the section Reading a Model Code takes that further.

We'll know how long it lasts when the first one wears out.Toyota slogan for the F engine, as quoted by Toyota UK [TUK-HD]

The 1HZ is an interference engine. Its camshaft is driven by a timing belt [T-ENG][W-HZ], and in an interference engine the valves and pistons share the same space at different moments. If the belt breaks or slips, they collide and the valves bend. This is a standing rule of this library; any document that says otherwise is in error. Our own belt history and replacement plan live in LC76_Facts.txt §4, not here.

Two engines deserve a word of their own. The 1HZ, introduced in August 1990 [T-ENG], is the plain one: no turbocharger from the factory, indirect injection, and a mechanical injection pump [T-ENG][W-HZ][CG-80]. Its ratings vary by market and year — 96 kW is the figure used here [W-HZ] — and it is still in production for the 70 Series in some markets, South Africa among them [W-HZ][T-ZA26]. Its simplicity is the whole point: it can be understood, and mended, far from a dealer. (MPa, in the 1HD-FTE row, is a unit of pressure: one MPa is ten bar.)

The 1HD line is the 1HZ's turbocharged, direct-injection cousin, and it grew more sophisticated in three steps: the 12-valve 1HD-T of 1990, the 24-valve 1HD-FT of 1995 and the electronically injected 1HD-FTE of 1998 [W-HD][T-ENG]. The last of these is the "FTE" engine often talked about in 70 Series circles; in the 70 it ran from 1998 to 2007 without an intercooler [W-HD][W-J70]. The step from the mechanical 1HZ to the electronic 1HD-FTE, and on to the common-rail 1VD-FTV, is the step from an engine you fix with spanners to one you fix with a laptop — neither better nor worse in itself, but a real choice for anyone travelling far from help.

Around the World

The Land Cruiser was an export vehicle almost from the start, and several countries built their own. What follows is by region, with South Africa and the rest of Africa given most room, because that is where this library is written and where the vehicle's reputation was most tested.

Brazil. Toyota do Brasil was founded in 1958, and its first vehicle, the Bandeirante, appeared in May 1959 — "Toyota's first overseas production vehicle", in Toyota's own words [TT-BR]. A full vehicle plant opened at São Bernardo do Campo in 1962, and it had built more than 100,000 Bandeirantes by 2001 [TT-BR], carrying the 40 Series shape to the end [W-J40]. Wikipedia's account dates Brazilian production of the FJ25 to November 1962 and the Mercedes-Benz diesel to December 1962 [W-J40]; the 1959 date is Toyota's and is used here. The plant closed in November 2023 after sixty years [TT-BR].

Venezuela and Colombia. At Cumaná, Venezuela, the 40, 60, 70 and 80 were assembled [W-J40][TUK-SW][LCHM-80]. Local names were affectionate: blog-level sources record the 70 as the "Machito" and the 80 as the "Autana" [BLOG][FAN]. In Colombia the 70 Series was built by SOFASA at Envigado from 1993 to 2008 [W-J70].

Portugal, Italy and Spain. Salvador Caetano, at Ovar in Portugal, built 70 Series vehicles fitted with Italian VM Motori 2.5-litre diesels, four- and five-cylinder, for Portugal, Italy and Spain from 1987 to about 1992 [W-J70].

South Africa. The first Toyota ever exported to South Africa was a Land Cruiser, in 1959 [W-TSAM], and Toyota confirms that Land Cruiser exports to South Africa, Nigeria and Angola began in the late 1950s [T-AFR]. Local assembly began in 1962, but of other models — the Stout, and later the Hilux and Corolla; the Prospecton plant near Durban builds Hilux, Fortuner, Corolla Cross and Hiace, not Land Cruisers [W-TSAM][T-AFR]. One local chapter is less clear. Wikipedia lists a South-Africa-only 3.9-litre four-cylinder diesel, the ADE 236, among the 70 Series engines [W-J70]. It was built by Atlantis Diesel Engines, a state-backed company set up after the 1977 United Nations arms embargo and licensed in 1979 [W-ADE]. A forum source says it went into the 75 in 1986–87, with the 1HZ taking over from 1988 [4X4C]; another source says these vehicles were assembled in Portugal [IDA]. Neither is confirmed by Toyota, so both are reported. Today, according to the motoring press, South Africa is among the Land Cruiser's ten biggest markets [CARS-FJ], and its 2026 70 Series range is the 76 wagon and the 79 single and double cab, with the 4.0 V6 petrol, the 4.2 1HZ and the 2.8 GD-6 [T-ZA26].

Kenya. Land Cruiser assembly began in Kenya in 1977 [T-AFR].

Australia. Australia is a core 70 Series market — the Troop Carrier, the 79 ute and the 76 wagon [T-AU]. It was the first market for the single-turbo V8 in 2007 [W-VD] and the market where the V8 ended in 2024–25 [T-AU]. For the seventieth anniversary Toyota Australia built just 600 special-edition 70s — 320 double cabs, 200 single cabs and 80 wagons — arriving in September 2021 [T-AU70].

The Middle East. Saudi Arabia was one of the first two export markets, in 1955 [TUK-HD]. Petrol engines dominate there, and the 300 Hybrid was launched there first [CS-300H][AI-300H].

Japan. The home market let the 70 go in 2004 [T-HD], took it back for a year in 2014 [T-70-30], and brought it back for good in 2023 [T-70JP].

The United States and Canada. The Land Cruiser was sold in the United States from 1958 and was the best-selling Toyota there from 1961 to 1965 [RLLC]. From the 60 onwards, American buyers had petrol engines only [JDM-GEN]. The name was absent for the 2022 and 2023 model years and returned in 2024 on the 250 [TS-US][EDM].

Europe. Official sales in the United Kingdom began in 1975 [TUK-HD]. Local names there included the Colorado (Prado 90) and the Amazon (100) [TUK-HD][TUK-SW].

The aid workers' vehicle. White 70 Series Land Cruisers are a familiar sight wherever the United Nations and aid agencies work. Many come through Toyota Gibraltar Stockholdings, a Gibraltar-based Toyota dealer that supplies and adapts them — chiefly 70s with the 4.2-litre non-turbo diesel — for the UN, non-governmental organisations and aid agencies [TG-TGS][W-HZ].

Lexus. The Station Wagon line has Lexus twins — the LX 450 (80), LX 470 (100), LX 570 (200) and LX 600 (300) [W-LX] — and so does the Prado: the GX 470 (120), GX 460 (150) and GX 550 (250) [W-GX].

The Toyota War 1986 – 1987

Part of the reason the Land Cruiser is known across Africa is a war. The conflict between Chad and Libya from December 1986 to September 1987 is called the "Toyota War" after the Toyota Hilux and Land Cruiser pickups that Chadian forces used [W-TW]. Chad's forces had French support, under Opération Épervier [W-TW].

The turning point was the Battle of Fada on 2 January 1987. Chad's force was the larger there — about 3,000 against about 1,500 [W-FADA] — and, according to Wikipedia's summary of the battle, Chadian troops in Toyota pickups destroyed 92 Libyan tanks for the loss of 18 dead and 3 trucks [W-FADA]. The speed and reach of light, reliable vehicles counted for more than armour on that ground. According to American sources, Libya lost about 7,500 killed and roughly US$1.5 billion of equipment over the war, and Chad about 1,000 killed [W-TW].

The war made the armed pickup — the "technical" — a fixture of later conflicts [W-TECH][SG], and Sudanese rebels later nicknamed the Darfur conflict the "Land Cruiser War" [W-LCW]. This is recorded here as history, without pride in it. A vehicle built to go anywhere and come back will be used for every purpose people have, the worst included.

Reading a Model Code

A Land Cruiser's model code looks like shorthand, but it can be read. J is Toyota's family letter for the Land Cruiser — which is why the whole 70 family is also called the "J70" [W-J70]. The letters before the J name the engine the vehicle was built with: HZJ means a 1HZ, HDJ a 1HD, VDJ a 1VD, FZJ a 1FZ and GDJ a 1GD [APW][W-J70]. The two digits after the J name the series and body, as described under the 70 Series [W-J70].

Worked examples HZJ76 — 1HZ diesel · Land Cruiser · 76 four-door semi-long wagon VDJ79 — 1VD V8 diesel · Land Cruiser · 79 long-wheelbase pickup HDJ80 — 1HD turbo diesel · Land Cruiser · 80 Series wagon FZJ80 — 1FZ petrol six · Land Cruiser · 80 Series wagon HJ61 — H-family diesel (the 12H-T) · Land Cruiser · 60 Series wagon

The old codes follow the same logic with a single engine letter: the BJ was the B engine in a "Jeep" [TUK-HD], and an HJ carries an H-family diesel [W-H]. The letters that follow the digits on a full code — body, trim, market — vary by market and are not covered here; for our own vehicle they are recorded in LC76_Facts.txt §1.

One puzzle is worth knowing about. Toyota dates the 76 four-door wagon to the 2007 facelift [T-HD], but Wikipedia also lists the 77 and 76 as semi-long four-door wagons offered in a few markets [W-J70]. The safest reading is that the 76 most people mean — the modern four-door wagon — is the 2007 model, and that earlier semi-long wagons carried the 77 code.

§ Where Our 76 Sits

Our vehicle is an HZJ76: a 1HZ diesel in the four-door semi-long 76 wagon, from the years just after the 2007 facelift that created that body. In the family tree it sits on the Heavy Duty branch — descended from the 40 and, before that, the BJ — with the plainest of the diesels under the bonnet. That combination is not an accident of the showroom. It is the Land Cruiser's oldest idea, kept deliberately simple, carried into the present.

This document holds no figures about our own vehicle. Everything about it — identity, weights, odometer, service history, tyres, electrics, and the timing-belt history of an interference engine — lives in one place, LC76_Facts.txt, the library's master of vehicle figures. How the 1HZ works, and how to look after it, is covered in the 1HZ Engine Training guide. Why we chose to travel this way is in the Manifesto.

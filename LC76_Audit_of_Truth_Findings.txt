▌1. WHAT WAS DONE, AND WHAT THIS IS

The register has audited this library three times and has never itself been
audited. It has been the auditor, never the audited. The Audit of Truth
closes that gap. Sessions 0–3 established the population by sweep rather than
assumption (not a sample — 100% coverage, charter M1), mapped how items are
BORN as well as how they are tracked, and tested every open item and every
numbered closure against the ten assertions.

This document consolidates that fieldwork into one findings report for owner
ruling. It does NOT remediate. Remediating while testing destroys the record
of what was wrong, and the sessions of 29 July showed what happens when
building runs ahead of checking. Remediation is Session 5, and only against
the rulings recorded here.

THE RATING BASIS IS NOW FINAL (updated 30 Jul 2026). M5 — the R50,000 cost
threshold — is RETIRED (owner ruling D1): cost and quantum are not rating
inputs, materiality is consequence (M1–M4, M6). The ratings below stand on
consequence alone. Two items were re-rated on that basis: OI-62 and OI-63,
MODERATE → SIGNIFICANT (ruling D2). See the RULINGS block at the end of §8.

bundle work that is done elsewhere. These are integrity defects in the
instrument, not missing obligations.

▌4. THE TWO NUMERIC RECONCILIATIONS (OI-62, OI-63)

These are carried out of the SIGNIFICANT group deliberately, because their
rating turns on the M5 ruling (see D1/D2). Both were tested by re-running the
service ledger, not by reading the register — all three inputs of each "overdue
by X km" claim were checked, not just the result (charter F-I). The scripts are
recorded in AoT_S2_A3_Recon and reproduce on demand (E2).

  OI-62  COOLANT CLUSTER. "Coolant flush ~86,006 km overdue" is arithmetically
         the ELAPSED distance since the only recorded flush (310,412 − 224,406,
         exact), carries NO service interval, and reads "~85,000 ago" in Facts §3
         — the same event, two numbers, two labels. The one-flush, no-thermostat,
         no-water-pump sub-claims are all TRUE against the ledger. The finding is
         that the QUANTUM is untraceable: a top-up is not a flush, and nobody can
         currently say how overdue the flush actually is.

  OI-63  WHEEL-BEARING REPACK. "~63,684 km overdue" recomputes only to a PHANTOM
         as-at of 308,684 (no such odometer reading exists), subtracts no interval
         despite the word "overdue", and IGNORES the Feb-2025 @ 271,200 km
         bearing replacement the ledger confirms — on which the overrun is
         ~19,800 km, not 63,684, an ~3× overstatement. Facts §3 again disagrees
         ("~65,000 ago").

NEITHER UNDERLYING JOB IS ASSERTED UNNECESSARY. Both a coolant flush and a
bearing repack plausibly justify workshop spend. The finding is that the
figures the register uses to justify that spend are wrong or untraceable, and
that Facts §3 carries a THIRD set of numbers for the same two events (see CC-6).
A wrong "overdue by X" figure drives premature or deferred spend and erodes
trust in every other maintenance number in the register.

RATING DIVERGENCE TO RULE (D2): the charter's interim F-I rated these
SIGNIFICANT ("both figures justify workshop spend"); the Session-2 matrix rated
them MODERATE (each individual job is well below the R50,000 M5 threshold). The
divergence is real and turns on M5: if untraceable maintenance quantum is
significant regardless of per-job cost — because it recurs across the whole
maintenance programme and it is the register's credibility at stake — they are
SIGNIFICANT; if M5 governs strictly and each job is sub-threshold, MODERATE.

RULED (D2, 30 Jul 2026): SIGNIFICANT — on integrity and schedulability, not
quantum. The register cannot say when these safety-relevant jobs are due and
three instruments disagree on the same event; that is the significance. Cost and
quantum are removed as rating inputs (M5 retired, D1). Both jobs will be done
regardless of cost or quantum — an operational certainty, recorded here, not a
rating input. Remediation: establish the true traceable interval at the
next-service workshop assessment, and reconcile Facts §3 into the OIR so one
master holds the number.


▌5. THE CLOSED-LOG FINDING (A10)

The closed log holds 61 entries; 27 close a numbered item. Each was tested
against A10 — does the closure name evidence meeting §8 for the item's type?
Full matrix: AoT_S3_ClosedLog.

THE CHARTER'S OWN INTERIM COUNT DOES NOT REPRODUCE. Charter §14 F-D asserts
"seven closures cite no evidence; 20 name something checkable." That figure is
UNLISTED (F-D names none of the seven) and cannot be reconstructed under any
consistent reading:

  · Under A10 AS WRITTEN (a closure fails only if closed on pure narrative),
    25 of 27 name a checkable artifact — a commit SHA, an HTTP-404 deletion
    check, a provider reference, a testzip, a char/byte reconciliation, or a
    dated owner ruling. Only TWO are weak: OI-49 (its closing state is a
    project-knowledge removal that lives outside the repo and cannot be verified
    at a commit) and OI-40 (its provider source is named by CLASS, not by an
    openable locator). That is 2 failing, not 7.

  · Under the STRICT §8 reading (a T5 build closure needs a NAMED COMMIT and
    RE-RUNNABLE validation, and "validation clean, depth walks 0/0/0" is E5
    unless the script and output are recorded), only 15 of 27 are clean and
    about ELEVEN rest on validation narrative with no commit SHA. That is ~12
    weak, not 7.

F-D's "7" matches neither bound. It is itself an E5 count — an assertion, in
the charter, of the exact kind the programme exists to remove. This is the
sharpest single vindication of the audit: the instrument that measures evidence
carried an unevidenced measurement.

RECOMMENDATION (D4). Withdraw F-D's "7 of 27" and replace it with the two-tier
result above. Then rule which tier becomes the CLOSURE STANDARD going forward:
if the strict reading is adopted (a closure must name its commit and a
re-runnable check), roughly eleven past closures are DONE-PENDING-EVIDENCE
rather than CLOSED, and every future closure carries that bar. This is the
highest-leverage ruling in the document, because it sets the standard the
Session-5 validator will enforce.

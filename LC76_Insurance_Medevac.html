Medical Advice — Not Rescue
Most medical calls are for advice, not an aircraft. A fever, a wound getting worse, an animal bite, a chest injury: a doctor on the phone can say whether to reach a clinic tonight or tomorrow, what to use from the kit, and when to call for evacuation. Decide now who you ring.

1. A medevac control room. AMREF's Maisha plan lists 24/7 medical advice for members. Whether the other candidates offer advice by phone is not yet checked (2 Nov 2026) — ask each one before choosing (§10, OI-43).

2. A doctor who knows you, agreed before departure. Doctor: ____________ · Number (full international format): ____________ · Best hours: ________ — carry it on the field card (§8).

When you ring: say “medical advice” (R10 §9 call script), then where you are, who is ill, temperature, pulse and oximeter reading (R10 §10 kit), and what you have given and when. A video call needs a data link (cellphone or Starlink — R16); over the satphone it is voice only.

Basis: an emergency physician's practice of advising remote crews by video (Dr Jon Solberg, XOVERLAND podcast EP79); owner ruling 2 Nov 2026.
3 — SOS Devices: inReach Mini 2 & IsatPhone 2.1

Two satellite devices on two independent networks. Only the inReach has an SOS button that reaches a rescue centre; on the satphone you dial the provider yourself. In a serious emergency use both.

Garmin inReach Mini 2 — the SOS button NEEDS AN ACTIVE PLAN

How it works: Press SOS, confirm, and after a 20-second countdown the alert goes over the Iridium network to Garmin's 24/7 rescue coordination centre (IERCC) with your position. The centre texts you, then hands the case to the most suitable local emergency service or medevac provider. It coordinates; it does not fly anyone or pay for anything.

Subscription: SOS works only on an active plan. A suspended plan disables SOS completely — re-activate at least 48 hours before departure and check before every remote leg.

Profile: Keep the emergency contact, blood type and medical details current in the Garmin Explore portal. The centre reads it when you press SOS.

Inmarsat IsatPhone 2.1 — the voice channel DIAL THE PROVIDER YOURSELF

Making the call: Extend the antenna with a clear view toward the equator and dial the provider's full international number from §2. Handset steps: R43.

Why it matters: You speak to a person. In a crisis voice is faster and clearer than text.

Airtime: A call uses prepaid units. Keep the SIM and the airtime live, and re-check after every top-up or plan change (R16 §6 item 7).

The satphone has no SOS for us. It is not registered with Garmin's rescue centre (owner decision, 8 Aug 2026), and its Assistance button is not configured — whether to set it, and to which number, is open (OI-3). Do not press it expecting a rescue response.

Confirm it is a genuine emergency needing outside help
An SOS starts a real search and rescue response. A stuck vehicle with nobody hurt is a recovery job (R9), not an SOS.
2
Press SOS on the inReach and confirm on the screen
20-second countdown with alarm. Tracking then runs every minute for 10 minutes, then every 10 minutes. Keep the device on and in open sky.
3
At the same time, ring the provider for where you are on the satphone
Use §2 “Who to Ring”. International numbers only. Have ready: GPS position (from the inReach screen), number of people, what happened, the nearest landmark or track name.
4
Answer Garmin's texts on the inReach
Confirm the type of emergency, number of people, medical condition and whether you can get yourselves out. Keep it short — messages are slow. Garmin can coordinate with the provider you rang.

If the provider rings back, brief them
Full name, GPS position, number of people, nature of the emergency, condition of the injured, and the satphone number. Keep the handset on, antenna up, clear view of the sky.
6
Stay put, stay visible, save battery on both devices
Rescuers come to your position. Move only if you are in immediate danger. Mark the spot — mirror, bright cloth, smoke.
7
Cancel the SOS only when it is fully over
Cancelling ends Garmin's tracking. Tell both Garmin and the provider you rang.

4 — Claims in the Field

A claim filed without the right papers is a denied claim. These steps apply while it is happening, not when you are home.

A — Vehicle Accident, Damage or Theft
1
Safety and injuries first
People before the vehicle. If anyone is hurt, R10 first.
2
Photograph everything before anything is moved
All vehicles, all damage, the road surface, skid marks, positions, weather. GPS position. Date-stamped. You cannot go back for more.
3
Get a police report — every claim needs one
Go to the nearest station. Get the reference number and, if possible, a stamped copy. At the scene, note the officer's name and number.
4
Take the other party's details — do not admit fault
Name, registration, insurer, phone, witnesses. Be polite and cooperative, but do not say “sorry” or “my fault” — it can bind you under local law.
5
Ring Cross Country straight away
+27 11 966 5004 from the satphone or abroad. Note the time, the person and the claim reference. Late notice can lose the claim, and assistance not arranged through the centre is at our cost.
6
No repairs until the insurer approves in writing
Emergency safety work (securing a broken window) is usually fine. Get major repairs approved by WhatsApp or email first. Keep every invoice.
7
Written off or stolen abroad — the Carnet liability does not die with it
A vehicle on a Carnet de Passages stays a customs liability as a wreck. It must be disposed of under customs supervision with papers, re-exported, or duty paid — otherwise the bond is called. Get the customs disposal certificate before leaving the country and tell the Carnet issuer at once. Settling the claim does not settle customs (§5, R39).
B — Medical Treatment
1
Treat first, paperwork later
Get to the nearest capable facility. Tell the medical aid or insurer once the patient is stable — most want notice within 24 hours of admission.
2
Pre-authorise anything that is not an emergency
Ring the Bonitas / AZOZA travel desk (+27 11 991 8174) or the travel insurer before admission. A payment guarantee means the hospital is paid directly. Bonitas needs pre-authorisation above R5,000 for direct payment — confirm the travel insurer's threshold once one is chosen.
3
Keep every paper
Every receipt, doctor's note and invoice, in the original language with an English translation if you can get one. Photograph them. A missing receipt is a denied line.
4
Small costs: pay and claim later
Below the authorisation threshold, pay and keep receipts. Bonitas wants claims within 4 months, with patient name, diagnosis, provider, date, treatment and cost.
C — Medical Evacuation
1
Is this a medevac?
Life-threatening condition or serious injury that local care cannot handle; getting worse; cannot safely go by road. If unsure, ring for advice (§2).
2
Ring the provider for where you are, on the satphone
§2 “Who to Ring”. Have ready: patient name, membership or policy number, GPS position, condition, treatment so far, nearest airstrip, a callback number.
3
Tell whoever pays
Cross Country if inside its limits; otherwise the expedition medical insurer once chosen. Get a claim reference before the flight if you possibly can.
4
Get to the airstrip
Fixed-wing air ambulances, including AMREF, need a serviceable airstrip, not an open field. The provider names the nearest one and the timing. Helicopter landing rules apply only to South African helicopter services in Southern Africa.
5
The partner who stays: vehicle, camp and family
Secure the vehicle and camp, keep the documents, tell next of kin, and arrange the vehicle with Cross Country. The power of attorney each holds for the other is what makes this workable (§8).
Lessons That Decide Claims
  • List the whole build with the insurer. Accessories are covered only once a full list with values has been submitted (§5). An unlisted fitment is unpaid; an undeclared one can void the policy.
  • Police report + photographs + pre-trip inventory = a payable claim. All three take a day to prepare before departure.
  • “Unattended” and “unlocked” are standard theft denials. Lock the vehicle; keep cameras and electronics on you or locked away out of sight.
  • The medical-aid travel benefit stops at its day limit. Treatment on the day after is not paid. Check your position at day 85 (§8).
  • An SOS is for life, not for a stuck vehicle. A rescue launched for a recovery job may not be paid by any policy. Work the recovery kit first (R9).
5 — Vehicle Cover: Cross Country

Cross Country Domestic 2.0, policy C016591, underwritten by Renasa, from 14 Aug 2026. Broker: Geoff Joubert, Umhlanga Insurance Brokers (Facts §10). Assistance numbers: §2.

Overloading voids the cover — and the vehicle is over GVM at full trim. An assessor who weighs a damaged vehicle above its 3,000 kg plated GVM repudiates the claim, whatever caused the incident. The weighbridge (10 Aug 2026) put the fully provisioned vehicle at 3,280 kg — 280 kg over GVM. Each axle is legal on its own (rear 2,120 kg of 2,180; front 1,160 kg of 1,480). Burning a tank of fuel and water sheds about 180 kg, so it drops under GVM mid-leg and goes over again at every fill. Making it legal is a decision — shed payload or a certified GVM re-rating (OI-103). Keep the weighbridge slip with the policy. Figures: Facts §1.
What the Vehicle Is Insured For

Agreed value R806,847 (policy schedule). A total loss pays the agreed value or the retail value at the time of loss, whichever is less — so keep the schedule's mileage current at each renewal, or a slump in retail value can settle below the agreed figure. Accessories and fitments are covered at agreed value, but only once a full list with values has been submitted; the Build Register is that list.

Conditions That Bite
  • Cross-border in the first 3 months (before about 14 Nov 2026) means 3 months' premium up front, and border confirmation only after proof of payment.
  • Vehicle repatriation from outside South Africa is capped — R30,000 after a breakdown, R65,000 after an accident, for a 4x4. Recovering a disabled vehicle from remote Zambia or Tanzania may cost more; the shortfall is paid in cash on the road. Owner action: get one written indicative quote from a commercial recovery operator.
  • Its medical evacuation has a hard limit. Full benefit in South Africa and the near neighbours (Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Lesotho, Eswatini). In Angola, Malawi, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya and Uganda it falls under the international personal accident and medical benefit: 30 consecutive days out of South Africa, no one over 75, motor homes excluded. Useful, but not the evacuation cover to rely on north of the Zambezi. Both crew must stay under 75 for the whole expedition.
  • Overloading — see the box above.
A Total Loss Abroad — the Carnet Problem
Two masters with opposite interests. On a write-off outside South Africa the insurer wants the cheapest disposal, usually local salvage. The Carnet de Passages needs the vehicle to leave the country or be destroyed under customs supervision, with papers — or the bond is called, personally, against the Carnet holder. Settling the claim does not end the customs liability. Before departure, get in writing whether Cross Country will pay for customs-supervised destruction or export. It cannot be answered at the roadside. Field steps: §4 A step 7.

  • Is damage off a proclaimed road covered? Most of the route is off proclaimed roads. This one could make the whole policy useless.Confirm first

6 — Third Party & the Yellow Card

Driving without valid third-party cover is illegal and leaves you personally liable if you injure someone or damage their property. It is the legal minimum at every border, and it is not part of the Cross Country policy.

COMESA Yellow Card KEY DOCUMENT

What it is: A regional third-party scheme recognised in 13 countries: Burundi, DR Congo, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Malawi, Rwanda, Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Djibouti. South Africa is not a member.

Covers: Injury and property damage to others, and emergency medical costs for the vehicle's occupants after a road accident — to each country's legal minimum. Not our own vehicle, theft, or anything that is not a road accident.

Duration: 30 to 365 days. For two years, buy 365 days and plan one renewal on the road.

Where to buy: Mukfin Insurance, Randburg (+27 10 210 7093 or +27 11 886 0667), or in Zambia — Livingstone or Lusaka is the easiest and cheapest point on the route. It is issued as an add-on to an underlying policy.

Cost: A forum-reported price for a 4x4, all COMESA countries, 6 months, was about USD 57 (2026). Get a written quote.

Carry the original in the vehicle at all times, with a scan as backup.

10 — Decisions Still Open

Two choices are not yet made (OI-43): expedition medical insurance, which pays the hospital and brings us home, and a medevac membership, which sends the aircraft. Neil's Genesis gap is part of the first. Every provider below is a candidate, not a choice.

The Gap Each Choice Has to Fill
  • Hospital bills abroad. Neil has nothing outside South Africa. Mary-Ann has Bonitas for the first 60–90 days only. Cross Country's medical benefit runs 30 days north of the near neighbours, and no one over 75.
  • Getting to a hospital. Cross Country evacuates at full benefit only in South Africa and the near neighbours. Beyond that, after day 30, a flight is at our own cost.
  • Getting home. A medevac flight goes to the nearest capable hospital; repatriation to South Africa is the insurer's job.
Why both, not one. Insurance pays the bill; a membership sends the aircraft. With insurance alone, the money exists but the insurer must first arrange a flight, with approvals and delay. With a membership alone, the aircraft comes but you may arrive at a hospital you cannot pay. If no long-stay policy will accept us at our ages, the plan does not get adjusted — it is rebuilt around a medevac membership plus a self-funded medical reserve, which is a route and budget decision.
Test These Before Buying — Age First
1
Age acceptance
During a 2028–2030 trip we will be about 67–69 (Neil) and 72–74 (Mary-Ann). This is usually the binding limit, not price. Answer it early, not six months out.
2
Single-trip caps inside “annual” policies
An annual multi-trip policy often caps any one absence at 30, 60 or 90 days. An annual policy is not a two-year policy.
3
Hazardous-activity exclusions
Get it in writing that self-driven remote overland travel, recovery work and river crossings are covered.
4
Government-advisory changes
What happens if a country's travel advisory changes while we are in it — cover stops at once, on exit, or not at all?
5
Residence and return-home conditions
Some policies require periodic return to South Africa. A no-return trip can breach a condition nobody read.
Minimum for expedition medical insurance: medical cover of at least USD 500,000; evacuation and repatriation included; self-drive off-road, remote camping and water crossings covered; 12–24 months, or renewable on the road without returning; no exclusion for sub-Saharan Africa; a clear definition of remote-area cover; political evacuation as an option. Also ask whether a doctor can be consulted by phone (§2).
